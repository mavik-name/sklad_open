## CENTRAL CONCEPT — HARD CANON

A generation ship travels through deep space from point A to point B.

The passage is so long that it may span several human generations. People are born, live and die aboard the ship while the vessel continues toward its destination.

The ship functions as a parable/analogue of Earth / the created world.

There is a ship's journal / instruction / inherited operational record that functions as an analogue of Scripture: it preserves how people should live and behave aboard the ship so that the passage can actually be completed and the vessel can reach its destination.

Some descendants continue to trust and follow the instruction.

Others increasingly ignore it because the ship appears to keep flying in the correct direction anyway. From their point of view, rules inherited from long-dead generations can look unnecessary, restrictive, outdated or superstitious.

## CENTRAL CONFLICT — HARD CANON

The conflict is NOT to be written as a literal simplistic “believers versus atheists” fight.

The parable operates through responsibility for a shared vessel and for people who will live after the current generation:

- one group understands that systems degrade, resources and maintenance matter, and inherited rules may encode consequences that are not immediately visible;
- another group lives primarily in the present, treats the ship's continuing motion as proof that the old instructions are unnecessary, mocks constraints and gradually neglects, damages or consumes the vessel's life-supporting order.

The ship continuing to move in the correct direction must remain an important temptation: immediate success can conceal long-term degradation.

## THEMATIC AXIS

The project should explore:
- inherited responsibility;
- freedom versus discipline;
- trust in knowledge whose original witnesses are long dead;
- the difference between “the ship is still flying” and “the ship will arrive”;
- responsibility to future generations who cannot vote in the present;
- whether rules remain meaningful when their consequences are delayed beyond one person's lifetime;
- faith not as decorative religiosity but as action based on a reality that is not always immediately testable.

The analogy must remain literary rather than turning into a theological lecture.

- ship name/model/size;
- propulsion technology;

- whether Earth itself still exists or what happened there;

- social/government structure;
- exact content, origin or authorship of the ship instruction;
- whether a captain/founder figure maps directly to God/Christ/Moses or anyone else;
- whether prayer has a literal technological analogue;
- exact nature and timing of system failures;
- whether the destination is reached;
- final religious/metaphysical explanation.

Keep these as open questions until the author fixes them.

## STYLE RULE

The science-fiction layer must work as a real world in its own right. Do not simply rename Bible terms as spaceship terms one-for-one.

The reader should be able to engage with the practical logic of the ship even without decoding every theological parallel.

Use concrete consequences, maintenance choices, generational memory, habits, conflict and lived decisions rather than explanatory author monologues.
