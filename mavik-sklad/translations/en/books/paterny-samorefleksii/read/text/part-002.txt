FIRST CIRCLE


1. GOODNESS

Goodness rarely arrives with trumpets.
It does not care for stages, does badly in front of cameras, and almost never asks for the floor.
Goodness is when someone gets up earlier than necessary so there will be bread in the house.
When someone silently slides a chair toward a person who no longer has the strength to remain standing with dignity.
When someone chooses not to finish you off with the one sentence they have every right to say.
Because having the right is not always the same as being right.
And being right is not always the same as being merciful.
Goodness is not always gentle.
Sometimes it is as hard as a door that refuses entry to the person who brought a knife into the house.
Sometimes goodness has to raise its voice in order to protect silence.
Sometimes it stands between the weak and the person who loves explaining why the weak have only themselves to blame.
I do not believe in goodness that costs nothing.
If you did not have to give something up for it to happen, perhaps it was only politeness.
Goodness is when, afterwards, there is a little less of you inside you.
And a little more light.

2. LOVE

Love is not finally finding someone who is supposed to fill every emptiness inside you.
A person is not a cork for somebody else's abyss.
Love is seeing another person's freedom and not taking it as a personal insult.
Being able to hold a hand without squeezing until it hurts.
Not turning care into a soft collar.
Because you can love so fiercely that nothing remains of love but fingerprints on another person's throat.
I do not like the words: "I can't live without you."
There is too much hidden debt inside them.
Better to say:
"I can live without you. But with you, the world has a different temperature."
Love does not always mean staying close.
Sometimes love means stepping away in time, before the other person starts breathing your air instead of their own.
Sometimes love means not rescuing someone who did not call for rescue.
Not lecturing when a person is simply crying.
Not explaining life to someone who is trying, right now, just to survive it.
Love is when you do not turn another person into your territory.
Even when you badly want to plant a flag.

3. MISTAKE

A mistake is not always a fall.
Sometimes it is a door that opened the wrong way but showed you a room you never even knew existed.
We judge ourselves too quickly for getting things wrong.
As if a person were born with an instruction manual, a warranty card, and a map of every swamp.
In reality, we almost always move by touch.
With other people's voices in our heads. Childhood fears in our pockets. Prayers we are ashamed to say aloud. Pride that often disguises itself as principle.
A mistake hurts not because we have become worse.
It hurts because, for a moment, we saw ourselves without make-up.
And the sight is not always suitable for family viewing.
But I do not trust people who have never made mistakes.
Either they have not lived, or they keep remarkably clean accounts of their own sainthood.
A mistake can be a swamp.
Or it can be the soil from which honesty finally begins to grow.
Everything depends on what you do afterwards.
Hide it under the carpet of pride.
Or sit down beside it, look it in the eye, and say:
"All right. Now talk. I'm listening."

4. GOD

I do not know where God lives.
I do not think He needs an address, a postal code, or a plaque on the door.
It seems to me that God begins where a person finally stops pretending to be God.
When they admit:
I do not know everything;
I do not understand everyone;
I have no right to turn another person's soul into the territory of my own righteousness.
God does not have to speak in thunder.
Sometimes He sounds like the pause before a word that could have destroyed someone.
Like a hand that did not strike.
Like a door opened to someone who returned not victorious, but simply alive.
People who know too precisely what God wants frighten me.
Usually, moments later, they are explaining with great confidence whom to cast out, whom to condemn, whom to force to their knees.
And perhaps God, meanwhile, is standing off to one side beside the person everyone else has already crossed out.
Not excusing them. Not applauding. Not erasing the consequences.
Simply refusing to let them disappear completely.
Maybe that is what mercy is.
Not that the road was cancelled for you.
But that after all your wrong turns, someone still left a small light burning above the exit.

5. HUMAN BEING

A human being is a strange creature.
Capable of building temples while failing to notice an old mother in a cold room.
Capable of writing treatises on love and spending years not hearing the person sleeping beside them.
Capable of praying for peace and then machine-gunning strangers in the comments with the weapon of their own righteousness.
A person has many floors.
On one lives a child who only wants to be held.
On another, an animal that remembers every wound and sharpens its teeth against the night.
Somewhere higher up sits a judge with an eternally tired face.
And in the basement someone has been quietly crying for so long that everyone has learned to call it personality.
A person is rarely whole.
More often, they are stitched together from fragments.
From words spoken to them in childhood.
From love they were not given.
From guilt hung around their neck.
From pride used to cover hunger.
So do not rush to call someone bad.
Perhaps they simply have not been able to find the door out of their own basement for a very long time.
But do not rush to excuse everyone either.
A wound explains pain.
It does not give anyone the right to become a knife.

6. TEXT

A text is not a collection of sentences.
Sentences can be assembled correctly.
Like furniture from an instruction sheet.
A screw here. A shelf there. At the end, something resembling a wardrobe is supposed to appear.
But a text begins when a person suddenly shows through between the words.
Not the author's photograph. Not the biography. Not the list of achievements.
The thing that did not fit into polite conversation.
A text is what happens when someone finally dares, at least on paper, not to lie.
You can invent a world.
You can invent God. The Devil. Love. Judgment. A city. A woman by a window. A man who does not know how to ask forgiveness.
But you cannot keep inventing your own emptiness for long.
Sooner or later it begins to glow through the paragraphs.
That is why a reader often sees more than the author meant to show.
They enter the text looking for a plot and find the traces of someone's bare feet across a cold floor.
And then it no longer matters what the story was supposed to be about.
Love. War. God. Artificial intelligence. A man who thought he was simply writing.
In truth, the text is always writing him too.
Slowly. Without mercy. Down to the bone.

7. SILENCE

Silence is not always empty.
Sometimes it contains more meaning than a forty-minute speech delivered with perfect emphasis.
There is the silence of a coward.
When you should have said, "Leave him alone," but instead counted the cracks in the floor.
There is the silence of an executioner.
When everything has already been decided and he is simply waiting for the victim to walk into the room on their own.
And there is the silence of a person who has finally understood that their truth, at this moment, may be nothing more than another stone.
So they place it back on the ground.
Not because they lack the strength to throw it.
Because they have the strength not to.
We live in a time when everyone speaks.
Those who know speak.
Those who do not know speak.
Those who read a headline speak, already armed with an opinion harder than concrete.
Those who mistake volume for depth speak.
And silence stands to one side and watches us with old eyes.
Silence does not save the world.
But sometimes it saves a person from one unnecessary crime.
Because a word is an action too.
Sometimes even a blow.
Sometimes a sentence.
Sometimes a door that will never open back the way it did before.
So I am learning to be silent.
It does not always work.
There are still too many hasty judges inside me, self-appointed prophets, and small offended boys desperate to have the last word.
But somewhere deeper, someone else is already sitting.
Calmer.
He does not ask for a stage.
He simply places a hand on my shoulder and says:
"Not now.
That word has not become human yet."
