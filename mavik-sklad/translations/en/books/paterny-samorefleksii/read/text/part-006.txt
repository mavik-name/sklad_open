FIFTH CIRCLE


29. OLD AGE

Old age is not when your knees hurt.
A young fool's knees can hurt too if he decided the body was a free rental with no contract.
Old age begins when you suddenly look into the mirror and see not yourself, but a person who has already returned many times from their own fires.
And did not carry everything out.
Some things stayed there.
In rooms you can no longer enter.
In conversations that never happened.
In doors slammed too loudly.
In words that should have been said while someone was still able to hear them.
Old age is when the past stops arriving like a film.
And starts arriving like an auditor.
It sits opposite you, puts its glasses on the table and says:
"Well? Shall we talk without music?"
And you no longer have the old agility for running away from yourself.
The body is slower.
Pride is more tired.
And the soul has seen too much to keep believing cheap excuses.
Old age can be frightening.
When a person turns into a heated warehouse of grievances.
When every day they take other people's guilt down from the shelf and polish it until it shines.
When the only thing still growing inside them is an account of who should still be ashamed, for what, and for how long.
But it can be different.
Not saintly.
Not a postcard with a wise grandfather under an apple tree.
Simply honest.
When a person finally stops competing with life for the last word.
When they learn not to throw every truth onto the table like a wet rag.
When they can say:
"I was a fool too.
Not always.
But often enough that I have no business turning myself into a monument."
I think a good old age is not a reward for a correct life.
Almost no one has seen a correct life up close.
A good old age is when a person is still able to become softer without losing their spine.
When their experience does not smell of mothballs and does not beat the young over the head.
When they can look at a world that no longer asks permission to change and not curse it merely for moving on.
Old age is the final edit.
You cannot change much of the plot anymore.
But you can still avoid ruining the last paragraph.

30. FATHERHOOD

Fatherhood is not biology.
Biology can make a person the cause of a birth.
It does not necessarily make him a home.
A father is not the person who knows loudest how everyone should live.
Not the man who walks around the house like a local god in a bad mood.
Not the one whose footsteps teach the children to predict today's climate.
Fatherhood is when another person can grow beside you, not merely adapt.
When your strength does not become weather everyone hides from in separate rooms.
When your voice does not turn the house into barracks with family photographs.
When a child does not have to earn the right to be held.
A father does not have to be perfect.
Thank God.
The perfect father is probably as dangerous an invention as the perfect righteous man.
You cannot live with him.
You can only hang him on a wall and dust him from time to time.
A living father makes mistakes.
Gets tired.
Sometimes speaks too sharply.
Sometimes fails to understand that what he calls a child's moodiness has long since become the child's first small despair.
But a living father can come back.
Not with a bag of gifts instead of an apology.
Not with the phrase: "I was doing it all for you."
He can simply take the crown off his head, set it down somewhere beside the shoes, and say:
"I was wrong.
Not because you won.
Because I saw."
Fatherhood is not ownership.
A child is not your project.
Not your thesis for the relatives.
Not proof that you left something behind.
A child is a separate human being whom, for some reason, God entrusted to you for the most vulnerable stretch of their road.
And if after you they are not afraid of their own voice, are not ashamed of their laughter, and do not confuse love with interrogation, then perhaps you did not ruin everything.
For a father, that is almost a sacred result.

31. MALE TENDERNESS

Male tenderness often wears a badly ironed shirt.
It does not know how to talk about itself very well.
It stands in the doorway.
Clears its throat.
Says something awkward:
"So... you... how are you, then?"
And already wants to run because there are too many exposed nerves in that sentence.
We were taught for a long time that a man should be stone.
Reliable.
Solid.
Silent.
The kind of stone people can lean on, put a bucket on, hang a coat from, wait out a storm behind, and never ask whether the stone itself is cold standing in the rain.
But a man is not stone.
Even if he worked very hard to look like it.
He has places where the skin is thin too.
Words he remembers for decades even though he looks as if he forgot them long ago.
Wounds he does not call wounds, because then it sounds as though one ought to sit down and have an honest conversation.
And that is practically a disaster.
Male tenderness does not always smell of flowers.
Sometimes it smells of repairs, smoke, a pharmacy, an old sweater, coffee he made too strong because he did not know how much sugar you take.
It may silently fix a cabinet door.
Carry a heavy bag.
Sit beside you in hospital pretending to read the news when in fact he is counting your breaths.
It rarely says:
"I'm afraid for you."
More often:
"Why are you out without a hat?"
And sometimes there is more love in that hat than in three volumes of correctly formulated feelings.
Male tenderness is afraid of looking ridiculous.
The world loves turning it into a joke.
But where a man is not ashamed to be tender without surrendering his strength, something rare begins.
Not heroism.
Not sainthood.
Simply a home where human warmth is not frightening.

32. BETRAYAL

Betrayal is not always a knife in the back.
Sometimes it is a small spoon used for years to scoop trust out from the inside.
Little by little.
Almost invisibly.
Today—you did not say.
Tomorrow—you hid.
The day after—you twisted it.
Then—you called it "I didn't want to hurt you."
And later still, it turned out the wound was already there.
It simply had not been allowed for a long time to call itself a wound.
What makes betrayal terrible is not that someone left.
People leave.
Love ends.
Roads part.
Sometimes an honest "I can't" hurts but does not destroy the architecture of the world itself.
Worse is discovering that for a long time someone lived beside you inside a counterfeit reality.
That while you were holding them, they were already carrying the most valuable things out of the house in their mind.
That your "we" had long since become merely a convenient household object to them.
Betrayal does not always begin with another person.
Often it begins with a small internal permission:
I am entitled to my secret baseness because I was hurt too.
That is where the door opens.
Not wide.
Just a little.
Exactly enough for darkness to get one finger through.
After that, darkness is very patient.
Betrayal changes more than relationships.
It damages a person's trust in their own perception.
Afterwards you test every kind word for strength.
Listen to pauses.
Study intonations.
Search warmth for the hidden temperature of future ash.
And perhaps that is the worst part.
The betrayer leaves.
But their shadow sits in your room for a long time afterwards, giving advice.
Betrayal does not need to be decorated with explanations.
Beautiful psychology does not make it nobler.
Yes, it may have reasons.
But a reason is not always an excuse.
Sometimes it is merely the address from which baseness came.
Betrayal is when a person kept their face before the world but lost their place in someone else's prayer.

33. PRAYER

Prayer is not always words.
Sometimes words are exactly what gets in the way.
They queue inside the mouth.
Push one another.
Dress in ceremonial clothes.
Try to look respectable before God, as if He chaired a committee for evaluating style.
Meanwhile, real prayer is sitting somewhere below the throat.
Without form.
Without grammar.
Without certainty it will be accepted at all.
Prayer can be very short.
Not "Lord, show me the way."
Simply:
"Well?"
And sometimes there is more faith in that "well?" than in the long speech of someone who has already answered themselves on heaven's behalf.
Prayer is when you finally stop pretending you control this entire strange construction called life.
When you come not with a report.
Not with a list of achievements.
Not with a complaint—though even a complaint sometimes has the right to stand by the door.
But with whatever is left.
Anger.
Fear.
Shame.
Stupid questions.
Exhaustion you are already ashamed to show other people.
Prayer is not always asking for a miracle.
Sometimes it is asking not to become a swine while the miracle has not happened.
Not to turn feral.
Not to simplify the world into a list of enemies.
Not to lose the ability to see a human being even in someone who worked very hard to resemble a verdict.
I do not think God needs our literary quality.
Perhaps honest hoarseness is closer to Him than a flawless sentence that has never once fallen to its knees inside.
So sometimes the most precise prayer is simply to be silent.
Breathe.
Not run away.
And keep a small patch of space inside you where light can still arrive, if for some reason it has not changed its mind.

34. CONFESSION

Confession is not an inventory of sins.
Not a list where each item needs a spiritual tick beside it.
Said it.
Repented.
Held the pause.
Moved on, neatly relieved, like after submitting a good report.
Real confession begins where a person stops editing themselves for God, for another person, for their own internal judge.
Because we often repent in such a way that we can still look like decent people trapped in difficult circumstances.
"Of course I'm guilty, but..."
That "but" is a small lawyer in a sinner's pocket.
Very hardworking.
Very educated.
Always ready to explain why everything was not so simple.
Sometimes it truly was not simple.
But some things become simpler when you switch off self-defence.
I lied.
I was afraid.
I used someone.
I stayed silent.
I wanted to look good in other people's eyes more than I wanted to be honest inside my own soul.
I called it love when it was fear of losing power.
I called it principle when it was resentment with good posture.
Confession hurts not because God suddenly learned the truth.
I do not think He is that naive.
It hurts because you finally heard the truth yourself.
Without music.
Without editing.
Without "you misunderstood me."
Confession is not humiliation.
It is a person's return from an underground war against themselves.
Sometimes it does not make things easier.
But it makes them more honest.
And honesty is not always light.
Sometimes, at first, it is merely switching off the fake lamp.

35. THE NEED TO BE HEARD

The need to be heard is not vanity.
Vanity wants a stage.
Being heard wants at least one living ear that is not preparing its answer while you are still trying to pronounce yourself.
A person can spend many years surrounded by conversations and never once be heard.
People listened.
Nodded.
Inserted examples of their own.
Gave advice.
Interrupted with: "That happened to me too."
And gradually the person learned to speak more briefly.
Then more briefly still.
Then stopped.
Not because there was nothing left to say.
Because they were tired of renting space inside somebody else's inattention.
To be heard is for your words not to fall into an empty shaft.
For someone not to rush to turn your pain into a task.
Not repair you like a chair with a loose leg.
Not sort you into categories in the first three minutes.
Simply hold a space in which you can finally finish speaking.
Finish speaking is an important phrase.
Because many people do not live with pain.
They live with broken-off sentences.
They were not heard to the end in childhood.
Not heard to the end in love.
Not heard to the end in anger.
Not heard when they could still have explained why that door should not be closed.
And now unfinished phrases walk around inside them like people at a railway station where every train has been cancelled.
Hearing a person does not mean agreeing.
It does not mean giving them the right to be right all the time.
It does not mean becoming a basket for somebody else's chaos.
To hear means to acknowledge:
you exist.
Your pain is not imaginary.
Your joy is not trivial.
Your voice is not background noise in my life.
Perhaps that is why a text sometimes saves more effectively than people do.
Paper does not interrupt.
Does not look at the clock.
Does not say: "Come on, everything will be fine."
It simply endures you.
And if you are lucky, one day somebody will hear you through that paper.
Not everyone.
Not the world.
Not a crowd.
One person.
And sometimes that is enough for someone not to disappear for a little longer.
