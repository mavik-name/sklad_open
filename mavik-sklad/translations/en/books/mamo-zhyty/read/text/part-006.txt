Chapter 21: An Unexpected Gift from the Sky
Damn horsefly, Serhiy thought, listening to the persistent metallic buzzing that tore through the silence of the scorched ravine.
A drone circled overhead like a predator scenting prey. His vision was already clouded by exhaustion and pain, but he kept trying to scan the sky and find the small lethal speck. From the way the sound rose and fell, it seemed the operator was trying to line something up, the machine almost "sniffing" through the air as it searched for a gap in the thick canopy of brush.
"Drop!" Serhiy shouted suddenly when he caught sight of something dark falling through the branches.
It did not move like a shell or grenade. It fell more slowly, almost gently.
The men flattened themselves instinctively against the earth and held their breath, preparing for the worst.
The package landed with a dull thump directly on the head of Vitko, the team's radio operator, who was lying closest to Serhiy.
Vitko, a young soldier with dirt and sweat covering his face, jerked in surprise. Then he opened his eyes, stared at the strange object, and cautiously picked it up.
Confusion crossed his face.
Then something almost childlike broke through it—pure joy.
"Holy shit!" he croaked, suddenly laughing. "It's ours! Boys, it's ours!"
Inside were water and medical supplies.
Two large bottles gleamed in the sunlight like precious stones. There were several individual first-aid kits, dressings, painkillers, antibiotics.
It was more than cargo.
It was a gift from the sky. A narrow beam of hope in a situation that had seemed finished.
It gave them a chance.
At the very least, it postponed the end.
Serhiy felt an enormous weight loosen on his shoulders. His heart, which had been hammering wildly, slowed a little.
This meant they had not been abandoned.
Someone knew they were here.
They were not alone in this hell.
He grabbed one of the water bottles and took several desperate swallows, feeling the moisture spread through his dry throat like life itself. Then he handed it to Maksym, whose face had turned green from thirst and blood loss.
"Quickly, boys. Dress the wounds. Drink."
His voice already sounded stronger than it had a few minutes earlier.
They moved fast but carefully.
Every mouthful of water felt like another mouthful of life. Every fresh bandage offered the possibility that the bleeding might slow, that the pain might ease enough to keep moving.
The unexpected delivery changed more than their physical condition.
It gave them back morale.
The belief that survival might still be possible.
Anna and Maryanchyk flashed through Serhiy's mind again, but now the thought of them was no longer only pain. It became determination.
He had promised to return.
And now, perhaps, he had been given a chance to keep that promise.

Chapter 22: New Life, Old Fear
Far away in Germany, at the quiet Schwarz estate, Anna could not settle herself.
Something vague and nameless had been gnawing at her all day, an anxiety that seemed to vibrate low inside her chest like bass frequencies too deep to hear properly.
She felt that something was wrong with Serhiy.
The intuition that had warned her before was screaming now.
His phone did not answer. The time by which his mission was supposed to have ended had long passed. She checked her messages every five minutes, but the screen remained empty, almost mocking her hope.
Maryanchyk, already preparing for school and full of the energy of a child who had begun returning to life, noticed her restlessness.
"Aunt Anya, why are you sad?" he asked, walking over and wrapping his arms around her leg. "Are you waiting for Uncle Serhiy? He said he'd come back."
Anna crouched and hugged him, forcing a smile.
"Yes, sweetheart. I'm waiting. He'll come back. He's just very busy right now."
"Will he find my mom?" Hope filled the boy's eyes. "And Dad?"
Her chest tightened.
She stroked his hair.
"He'll try very hard, sweetheart. But right now, how about we read your favorite book about dragons?"
She tried to bury herself in routine, but thoughts of Serhiy would not release her. They circled through her mind like black birds.
She remembered their nights together, full of tenderness and desire. His strong arms around her. His breath against her neck. His voice whispering words of love.
She remembered every touch, every kiss.
The memories were a blessing and a curse at once, because every one of them sharpened the fear of losing him.
And there was something else.
Something she had wanted to tell him today.
For several weeks she had noticed small things: light dizziness, nausea in the mornings, unusual fatigue. She kept blaming stress, pushing the thought away.
But that morning she had finally decided to stop wondering.
She bought a test.
Held her breath.
Waited.
There was a plus sign.
The positive test lay on the bedside cabinet like a tiny verdict carrying the weight of an entire life.
A plus.
She was pregnant.
Pregnant with Serhiy's child.
The news should have filled her with joy.
Instead she felt panic.
How could she bring a child into the world now, when the father might be somewhere in danger—or...
She could not finish the thought.
She had wanted to tell him in person. To see his face. To share the joy and fear with him.
Now she did not even know whether she would get the chance.
Anna went to the window and looked out at the dark German sky.
Somewhere under that same sky, thousands of kilometers away, was the man she loved.
The father of her unborn child.
And all she could do was wait.
Wait and pray.

Chapter 23: Waiting in the Ravine
The sun that had burned them without mercy only hours earlier was slowly sinking toward the horizon, throwing long distorted shadows across the scorched ground.
Serhiy's uniform was soaked with blood—his own and that of his men. As the least seriously wounded of the group, he forced himself to stay focused despite the throbbing pain in his shoulder and the thirst that still scraped through him.
The hands that had held a rifle only hours earlier now worked carefully over his soldiers' wounds.
Every movement sent sharp pain through his injured arm, but he ignored it and concentrated on keeping his men alive. He tore strips from his own clothing to supplement what remained of their dressings.
They had been lucky.
A fiber-optic reconnaissance unit had spotted them by chance.
That had become their salvation.
Unlike ordinary radio-controlled drones, the fiber-optic machines were not vulnerable to enemy electronic warfare. They emitted no control signal that could lead Russian reconnaissance back to the group's hiding place.
The enemy still did not know their exact location. They only suspected the wounded scouts were somewhere in the area.
The message came over the radio like fresh air.
A delay in the sentence.
"Hold on, boys," Serhiy said hoarsely as he tightened a dressing around the deep wound in Maksym's leg.
Maksym—the young soldier who had married only recently—was white-faced, his lips cracked from dehydration. He gave the smallest nod and clenched his teeth against a groan.
Serhiy could see the pain in him. The fear too.
But he could also see the will to live.
He remembered what it had been like to lie wounded himself, fighting for every breath. Right now the most important thing was to keep the men conscious and keep them from surrendering inwardly before help arrived.
The medication and dressings dropped by the drone gave them a chance.
At least a delay.
Serhiy administered pain relief to the worst wounded and did what he could to stabilize them.
He knew it was temporary. Their injuries needed real medical care. But these supplies might buy them several hours.
Maybe until dawn.
While they waited, his mind refused to rest.
Who was the mole?
Who had betrayed the raid and sent so many of his men into an ambush?
He went back through faces, conversations, fragments of the briefing.
Who had known?
Only a small circle of people in command.
Had anyone behaved strangely?
Someone too hurried?
Someone too calm?
Rage rose in his throat again.
This was not simply betrayal.
It was murder.
The murder of men who had trusted him and followed him into fire.
That pain cut deeper than the wound in his shoulder.
"The mole... he'll pay," Serhiy whispered, clenching his fist.
The thought became a new purpose.
A new mission.
He had to survive so he could find whoever had sold them out and answer for the men who had died.
Time dragged painfully.
Every minute seemed an hour.
The sun lowered, turning the sky crimson and orange.
After dark, an evacuation vehicle was supposed to move toward them.
They only had to hold on.
Hold on until night became their ally again, until they could move, until help arrived.
Again and again Anna and Maryanchyk came before his eyes.
Be careful.
Will you find my mom?
He had promised to return.
That was enough.
First he had to live until darkness.
Then he had to get his people out.
Serhiy looked at the exhausted faces around him.
They were placing everything they had left in him.
He could not fail them.
He had to find a way.
He had to bring them somewhere safe.
That was his duty.
That was the mission now.
And he would fight for it until his last breath.
