THE SCULPTOR
Viktor Makarchuk · MaVik

Chapter 3. Fund Meeting
Part 03

Petro stopped writing.

Kateryna looked from Marta to Lucas and back again.

Andrii lowered his head.

“Explain,” he said.

Lucas turned to him.

“Adults often come to children like these with very good intentions. To help. Teach. Support. But very quickly they begin to expect a result. For the child to become more open. Calmer. More grateful. Easier. In other words, not simply alive, but someone easier to love.”

Andrii said nothing.

Marta could see that her father was listening closely.

Too closely.

“But love should not demand a convenient form,” Lucas said.

Viktoriia slowly capped her pen.

The sentence hung over the table.

Marta felt something move sharply in her chest.

Not because of the children.

Because of her father.

Because of what he had said yesterday: if God had given me a son...

Andrii did not look away.

“You talk as if you’ve thought about this for a long time.”

“Longer than I would have liked.”

“Are you a man of faith, Lucas?”

Marta looked sharply at her father.

There it was.

The pastor’s question. It always sounded gentle, but in fact it opened a little door inside a person, one not everyone wanted a stranger’s hand reaching through.

Lucas took it calmly.

“I know faith exists.”

Petro snorted.

“Evasive.”

“Precise,” Lucas said.

Andrii did not smile.

“Knowing that faith exists and believing are not the same thing.”

“Yes.”

“So do you believe?”

Lucas set the marker on the table.

“I remember more than I believe.”

The room became very quiet.

The sentence was almost meaningless.

But it did not sound meaningless.

That was what unsettled Marta.

Viktoriia was the first to bring the conversation back to earth.

“Fine. We’re not getting to theology today, because we have a budget.”

Petro raised a hand.

“Finally, the voice of common sense.”

Kateryna exhaled as if she had not noticed she had been holding her breath.

Andrii looked at Lucas for a few more seconds.

Then nodded.

“Yes. Let’s get back to the program.”

Marta understood: her father was interested.

She did not like it.

Not because Lucas was dangerous. She still had no evidence.

But because Andrii loved people who knew how to speak about depth.

He believed that when someone touched pain precisely, they did it in order to heal.

Marta already knew: not always.

The meeting went on for almost an hour.
