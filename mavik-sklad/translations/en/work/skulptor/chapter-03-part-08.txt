THE SCULPTOR
Viktor Makarchuk · MaVik

Chapter 3. Fund Meeting
Part 08

Nothing mystical. No trace of anything. Just the hands of a person who knew how to work neatly.

“Why didn’t you defend yourself?” she asked.

“From what?”

“From me. At the meeting.”

“You weren’t attacking. You were checking.”

“That can be the same thing.”

“It can. But it wasn’t today.”

“You decide other people’s motives that easily?”

“No. Yours are not easy.”

She tensed.

“And what are mine?”

Lucas took his coat from the back of the chair.

“You’re not afraid of being wrong about me. You’re afraid of not being wrong.”

Marta felt the air grow denser.

“Explain.”

“If I’m dangerous, everything is simple. You’re right, you hold the boundary, you protect the children, yourself, your family. But if I really am helping, if the children around me aren’t breaking, if your mother wasn’t wrong, if your father isn’t naive, then you’ll have to admit something more difficult.”

“What?”

“That not every anxiety is a prophecy.”

She was silent.

Not because he had hit the mark.

Because he had hit it too cleanly.

“You do this all the time,” she said.

“Do what?”

“Take a person and press exactly where it hurts.”

Lucas did not look away.

“No.”

“No?”

“I press where the person is already holding a hand.”

It was cruel.

And perhaps that was exactly why it sounded almost gentle.

Marta stepped closer.

“Listen to me carefully. The children may like you. My mother may like you. You may say things that sound profound. But if I see you use a child, their fear, their trust, or their pain for yourself even once, I’ll stop you.”

Lucas looked at her for a long moment.

Then nodded.

“Finally.”

“What?”

“That was an introduction.”

Marta gave a short, joyless laugh.

“You’re unbearable.”

“Often.”

“And arrogant.”

“Less often than it seems.”

“I doubt that.”

“There are still many things you don’t think about me.”

He said it quietly.

Without a smile.

For the first time that evening, Marta felt not irritation.

Cold.

Not from outside. Not from the wet snow, not from an open door, not from exhaustion. Something else. The kind that comes when an ordinary conversation opens for a second over a drop far too deep beneath it.

“What is that supposed to mean?”

Lucas was already putting on his coat.

“Nothing you need to hurry with.”

“I don’t like answers like that.”

“I know.”

“Don’t say ‘I know.’”

He stopped by the door.

“All right.”

And left.

He did not slam the door.

Did not look back.

Left nothing behind except the closed box of markers on the table and a sentence Marta could not get out of her head.

She stood in the empty hall a little longer.

Then picked up her phone.

There was a message from Olena.

Gave Nazar the flashlight. He said he’ll return the batteries when he grows up. Then he fell asleep. With the flashlight under his pillow.
