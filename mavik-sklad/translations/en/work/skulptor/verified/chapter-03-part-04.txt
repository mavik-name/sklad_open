They agreed on the basic rules. Two volunteers at every session. No direct contact with the children outside the center. Materials transferred only through Olena or the administration. Photo reports without faces, names, or stories that could identify a child. Once a month, a joint review of needs.

Petro grumbled, but kept writing.

Kateryna suggested involving the older teenagers from the church, but Marta cut her off at once.

“Only after an orientation. And not all of them.”

“They want to help,” Kateryna said.

“Wanting to help isn’t enough. Sometimes it’s even part of the problem.”

Kateryna was not deeply offended, but visibly so.

Marta softened her tone.

“The children at the center are not practice equipment for our goodness. You can’t go there just to ‘try yourself in ministry.’”

Kateryna lowered her eyes.

“That’s not what I meant.”

“I know. But that’s what it can easily become if you don’t set a boundary.”

Andrii looked at Marta closely.

He was seeing more than his daughter in her. Right now, a professional.

And still, somewhere in his gaze, there was the same old expectation: be strong, explain, hold it together, don’t fall apart.

Lucas remained silent.

His silence was beginning to irritate Marta almost as much as his phrases.

When someone speaks with too much precision, it is dangerous.

When that same person stays silent at the exact moment they could win an argument, it is more dangerous still.

“Marta,” Viktoriia said, “can you prepare a short guide for the volunteers? No legal language. Simple points.”

“I can.”

“One page.”

“I’ll try.”

“And without making it sound as if every volunteer is already guilty.”

Marta looked at her mother.

“That will be harder.”

Viktoriia nodded.

“That’s why I’m asking you.”

Petro closed his notebook.

“I’ll have the budget calculated by Sunday. But I’m telling you now: if we want this to be regular, we’ll need dedicated donors.”

“No children’s faces,” Marta reminded him.

“Yes, yes. I wrote it down. So now our children are invisible, but happy.”

“Petro,” Andrii said quietly.

The deacon raised both hands.

“All right. I’m quiet.”

Lucas suddenly said:

“Invisibility is sometimes the first step toward safety.”

Petro looked at him.

“Do you always talk like that?”

“No.”

“Thank God.”

“Don’t be so quick.”

Kateryna snorted with laughter.

Even Viktoriia could not hold back a smile.

Marta did.

On principle.
