THE SCULPTOR
Viktor Makarchuk · MaVik

Chapter 2. Clay
Part 04

But the longer Marta watched, the harder it became not to see it: the children around him did not tense the way they did around most adults.

Not because he was kind. Marta was not ready to call him kind.

More because he was not trying to squeeze anything out of them.

At least not yet.

Nazar finally looked up.

“Can I make a flashlight?”

Lucas put a thinner wooden tool in front of him.

“You can.”

“It’ll be crooked.”

“Almost everything made by living hands is a little crooked.”

“The ones in stores are straight.”

“Stores are full of straight things. Not all of them are worth trusting.”

For the first time all morning, Nazar almost smiled.

Almost.

Marta felt it like a small blow.

Yesterday she had bought him time. Olena had given him safety. The fund had given him a flashlight. Serhii had given him procedure. And this man, with one sentence, had taken the shame out of making something crooked.

Unfair.

And very effective.

After the activity, the children carried their pieces to a shelf. Nazar held his clay flashlight in both hands. It did not look much like a real one: thick, uneven, with a handle that was too wide. But on the front Nazar had made a small opening.

“The light is here,” he explained to Olena.

“I see,” she said.

“You can’t see it. But it’s there.”

Marta turned toward the window.

She needed to pull herself together.

She could not let every sentence from a child pass through her armor. The armor did not make her callous. The armor was what let her go back to work tomorrow.

Lucas approached quietly, but not silently. She heard his footsteps.

“You’re angry,” he said.

“Is that an observation or a question?”

“A check.”

“Then the check failed.”

“No. The answer was simply predictable.”

Marta turned to him.

“You have a strange habit of talking as if people are problems to solve.”

“And you have one of talking as if every problem has a person hidden inside it.”

“Because usually it does.”

“That’s exactly why you’re here.”

She did not want this conversation.

But she was already in it.

“Have you been working with children long?” she asked.

“Long enough.”

“That isn’t an answer.”

“That is the boundary of the answer.”

“Volunteers in children’s centers would be better off with less mystery.”

“Agreed.”

“Then your last name.”

He looked at her attentively. Not as a challenge. More with interest, as if she had made an expected move that still mattered.

“It’s in the documents.”

“I’m not asking the documents. I’m asking you.”

The pause was brief.

“Martyn.”

“Lucas Martyn?”

“Yes.”

“Strange last name.”

“Ordinary enough not to draw attention.”

“And the first name is the opposite?”

“I was given my first name before I learned to think about convenience.”

He said it lightly.

But something clicked inside that lightness.

Marta did not understand what.

“Why were you outside the church yesterday?” she asked.

“I was picking up materials for the workshop.”

“From our fund?”

“From your mother’s fund.”

Marta did not show surprise.

At least she tried not to.

“You know my mother?”

“A little. Viktoriia Andriivna helps the center with materials. Clay, paper, paints. Sometimes clothing.”

“And she never told me you had an activity here?”

“Did you ask?”

No.

Marta had not asked.
