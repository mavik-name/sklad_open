Chapter 4. The Guide
Part 35

Marta did not get the chance to answer.

Lucas stood at another table, helping a child measure a piece of string for a pendant. He was not looking at them, but he said:

“Then there should be someone nearby who won’t laugh.”

Nazar thought about it.

Then very carefully ran a finger along the edge of the step, smoothing the unevenness.

Marta did not look at Lucas.

This time, not because she did not want to give him a victory.

Because the answer was right.

And for the first time, she no longer had the strength to be angry at something right simply because of who had said it.

That evening Marta came home with Nazar’s list in her bag.

She could have left it with Olena. Could have sent Serhii a photo. Could have said it was not her business.

But the sheet lay in the inner pocket, folded in half like a small document more important than most official ones.

At home she reheated the soup Viktoriia had once again sent through a volunteer she knew. It was thick, with buckwheat and potatoes. Marta ate standing by the stove, because sitting down would have meant admitting the day was over.

It was not.

Her phone lay on the table.

She picked it up several times and put it down again.

Then she finally opened a search and typed:

Lucas Andriiovych Martyn

Nothing substantial.

Volunteer program.

A few mentions in reports.

No social media.

No photographs of his face.

No past she could get hold of.
