Chapter 4. The Guide
Part 16

The metal rocket on the playground shone with water.

Olena met her in the lobby.

“Got them?”

Marta held out the folder.

“Ten copies.”

“Oh, you took this seriously.”

“I always take things seriously.”

“That’s exactly what scares me sometimes.”

Olena looked over the first page.

“Good. Plain language. No bureaucratic sludge. The last line is strong.”

“My mother said the same thing.”

“So I’m not the only smart one.”

Marta looked around.

“Where’s Nazar?”

“In the room. Drawing debts.”

“What?”

Olena nodded toward the corridor.

“Better see for yourself.”

Nazar sat at a small table by the window. A sheet of graph paper lay in front of him. The words were written in a column:

batteries
bread
chocolate
tea
police time
flashlight

The last word was crossed out.

Marta sat opposite him.

“Hi.”

Nazar looked up.

“Hi.”

“What’s this?”

“A list.”

“I can see that.”

“Things I have to return.”

“You crossed out the flashlight?”

“They gave it through the fund. Olena said it isn’t a debt.”

“She was right.”

“But the batteries are still there.”

“The ones you took from the store, yes. The ones in the flashlight are help.”

Nazar frowned.

“They’re the same.”

“No.”

“Why?”

Marta paused.

The question was simple.

The answer was not.

Because for adults the difference was procedural: one set of batteries was evidence, the other was charitable aid processed through the fund.
