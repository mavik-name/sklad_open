THE SCULPTOR
Viktor Makarchuk · MaVik

Chapter 3. Fund Meeting
Part 01

At seven o’clock the church no longer looked like a shelter after the rain.

In the evening it became a workplace.

In the small hall, the tables had been rearranged into a U. At one end lay lists, pens, folders with transfer forms, a box of cookies, and a thermos of tea. Several bags of children’s clothes stood against the wall, each with a slip of paper showing the size and age. Someone had put a flip chart in the corner, but the marker for it, as always, was being searched for by everyone at once.

Marta arrived out of uniform.

After the center she had stopped at home and changed into a dark sweater and jeans, but the feeling of the working day had not come off with her clothes. It sat somewhere deeper than her shoulders—in the way she looked at doors, at people, at who stood where and who moved too close to whom.

She stopped by the entrance.

Viktoriia, Andrii, Deacon Petro, and Kateryna, who ran the Sunday school, were already in the hall. Petro sat with a notebook and a calculator open on his phone. Kateryna was arranging colored sticky notes across the table with such seriousness that it looked as if the fate of the children’s program depended on the order of the notes.

Lucas stood by the flip chart holding a marker.

An ordinary black marker.

For some reason Marta noticed that immediately.

Not his fingers. Not his face. Not the dark sweater. The marker.

Because the marker meant his presence was not accidental. He had already been given a tool.

“Sweetheart, you came,” Viktoriia said.

“You asked me to.”

“Not as your mother. As the head of the fund.”

“That only changes the tone.”

Viktoriia gave the faintest smile.

Andrii stood up.

“Marta, I’m glad you’re here. We really do need your perspective.”

“As an officer or as your daughter?”

“Both, if you can handle it.”

She sat closer to the edge of the table, where she could see the door and Lucas at the same time.

Lucas noticed.

He did not comment on it.

And that was the best thing he could have done.

“Shall we start?” Petro asked.

Viktoriia nodded and opened the folder.

“Here’s the situation. The center is asking us to expand the workshop program. Right now they have clay once a week, sometimes drawing, sometimes clothes and stationery from us. Olena says the children need regular sessions, not one-off actions. Not ‘show up, hand things out, take pictures, leave,’ but an actual presence.”

“Regularity costs money,” Petro said.

“That’s exactly why you’re here,” Viktoriia replied.

“I thought I was here because I’m handsome.”

Without looking up, Kateryna said:

“Then you came to the wrong room.”

Andrii laughed softly.

Marta noticed that Lucas almost smiled too. Not broadly. Just at one corner of his mouth. Like someone who had allowed himself a brief pause without stepping out of his role.
