THE SCULPTOR
Viktor Makarchuk · MaVik

Chapter 4. The Guide
Part 01

The guide had to fit on one page.

That was Viktoriia’s requirement.

“Volunteers won’t read three pages,” she had written in a morning message. “And even if they do, they’ll forget half. Write it so a person understands before they have time to get offended.”

Marta read the message at the kitchen table, with a cup of coffee beside her and the laptop open in front of her.

A blank page blinked on the screen.

She had already written a heading:

Rules for Working with Children in the Workshop Program

And stopped there.

Not because she did not know the rules.

The opposite.

She knew too many of them.

Do not be alone with a child. Do not touch without need and permission. Do not give personal contact information. Do not accept secrets that involve danger. Do not promise what you cannot control. Do not ask “what happened to you?” just because you are curious. Do not call a child “poor thing.” Do not ask them to smile for a photo. Do not turn someone else’s wound into proof of your own goodness.

The problem was something else.

How do you write all of that without people hearing: you are potential abusers?

And at the same time make sure those who really could do harm do not find a gap in softer wording.

Marta deleted the heading.

Wrote a new one:

A Child Is Not Obliged to Trust You

She looked at it.

Too sharp.

Deleted it.

Wrote:

Boundaries That Protect Children and Volunteers

That was better.

Drier.

Less honest.

She leaned back in the chair.

Wet snow was falling outside. Down below, the janitor pushed slush along the curb with a shovel. Cars moved slowly, as if the city had not yet decided whether it had woken up or was simply continuing yesterday.

Her phone vibrated.

Serhii:

Battery confirmed. There’s a report from the garages. The stepfather says he “found it by the dumpster.” Very talented dumpster.

Marta let out a short breath.

Marta:

Will Nazar get in trouble over the theft?

The reply did not come immediately.

Serhii:

Formally, the paperwork exists. In practice—the child is in danger, the amount is small, the circumstances are obvious. It’ll be prevention work, child services, the family. Don’t turn this into a crime novel.

Marta looked at the screen.

Marta:

We’re already in a novel.
