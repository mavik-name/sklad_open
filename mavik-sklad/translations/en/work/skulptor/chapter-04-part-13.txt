Chapter 4. The Guide
Part 13

“Serhii, you haven’t seen him.”

“I’ve seen plenty of people I wish I hadn’t. Believe me, if a person is strange, that doesn’t automatically make them dangerous.”

“And if it does?”

“Then sooner or later they’ll make a mistake. Everyone does.”

“Not everyone.”

He looked at her more closely.

“Now that is a dangerous thought.”

“Why?”

“Because if you decide someone doesn’t make mistakes, you’ll either start fearing them or worshipping them. Both are bad options.”

Marta was about to answer when the duty officer came into the room.

“Kravets, someone’s here about the battery. Says he may be able to identify it.”

Serhii set down his cup.

“Back to work.”

He left.

Marta remained in front of the open document.

Lucas Andriiovych Martyn.

An ordinary name.

An ordinary file.

An ordinary certificate.

And still it felt as though she were looking not at an answer, but at a very well-made door.

No cracks.

No lock on the outside.

But a door.

She printed the guide before lunch.

One page.

Six points.

No accusations.

No moralizing.

Almost no fear.

Marta read it aloud to the empty office:

“A child has the right not to speak. A volunteer does not pull stories out of a child or ask questions that satisfy an adult’s curiosity but do nothing to help the child...”

She stopped.

The sentence was too long.

Deleted it.

Wrote it shorter:

Don’t interrogate. If a child wants to talk—listen. If they stay silent—respect it.
