EVACUATION, OR ON THE ROAD TO THE STABILIZATION POINT
Viktor Makarchuk · MaVik

Story Three

Doc

Ghost climbed out of the trench first.

Forty meters of open ground suddenly looked a lot longer than it had from below.

“Stay together,” he threw over his shoulder.

“We’re fucking trying,” Beard answered.

Pixel added something, but Ghost no longer caught it. The whine in his head had grown louder again.

The hand in the grass did not rise anymore.

“Friendly!” Ghost shouted. “Don’t shoot.”

“I’m already fucking sick of yelling that I’m friendly!”

The voice was closer than it had sounded. Near the edge of the tree line, the grass was flattened around a small crater. A soldier sat inside it. Not lying down—sitting, with his back against the earth. His rifle lay beside him.

Another man lay about a meter away.

Ghost dropped into the crater and understood at once there would be no questions for the second man.

The one sitting followed his gaze.

“That’s it.”

Ghost nodded.

“How are you?”

“Fucking wonderful.”

“Heard that one already today.”

“Must be a good day.”

A bandage had been wrapped around his right arm. Wrapped the way somebody might do it with his teeth. Blood had seeped through the gauze. His fingers were dirty, but they moved.

“Show me.”

“You a medic?”

“No.”

The man looked at him.

“Then why the fuck should I show you?”

Heavy breathing came from behind.

Beard and Pixel finally reached the crater. Pixel spotted the patch on the wounded man’s armor and smiled.

“Oh. He’s a medic.”

Ghost looked.

There really was a medical cross on one of the pouches.

“Even better. You can show yourself.”

The man dropped his chin to his chest.

“Doc.”

“What?”

“Call sign. Doc.”

Beard snorted.

“Big day for unexpected call signs.”

“And you are?”

“Beard.”

Doc looked at his beard.

“Right.”

Pixel raised a hand.

“Pixel.”

“Drone guy?”

“Used to fix phones.”

“Even better.”

Ghost crouched beside Doc.

“Wrapped the arm yourself?”

“Who else?”

“Shitty job.”

Doc slowly turned his head.

“Third time today I’ve heard that. You all from the same medical school?”

Pixel laughed.

“He criticized my tourniquet too.”

Doc immediately looked at his leg.

“How long has it been on?”

“Don’t know.”

“Fuck.”

“His phone showed 13:06 when we met,” Ghost said. “It was already on. I tightened it.”

Doc looked at him.

“Any bleeding after?”

“No.”

“Didn’t twist it, didn’t loosen it?”

“No.”

“At least you did something right.”

Pixel looked offended.

“I put it on myself in the first place.”

“Good boy. I’ll give you candy at the stab point.”

“They gonna cut the leg off?”

Doc looked at him for about two seconds.

“Yeah.”

Pixel froze.

“What?”

“The other one too. For symmetry.”

Beard roared with laughter and grabbed his side.

“Fuck… Doc, you’re gonna finish my ribs.”

Pixel stared at the medic.

“You’re fucked in the head.”

“Don’t diagnose the doctor.”

Ghost nodded at Doc’s right arm.

“What happened there?”

Doc grew more serious.

“Fragment. Forearm. Doesn’t feel like bone. Bled plenty. Less now.”

“Why are you sitting?”

Doc did not answer.

Ghost looked at the man beside him.

Doc did too.

“He was alive when I crawled here.”

Nobody said anything.

“Alive,” Doc repeated. “Ten minutes, maybe. I don’t know. Lost my phone somewhere. No watch.”

He wiped his face with his left hand.

“I did everything.”

Ghost nodded.

“Good.”

“No, fuck. Not good.”

“That’s not what I meant.”

Doc looked at him.

Ghost held out a hand.

“Let’s redo the dressing properly, then you come with us.”

“Where?”

Pixel answered first:

“To the stab point. Five kilometers that actually come in hundred-meter pieces.”

Beard nodded.

“And his hundred meters can be a hundred and seventy.”

Doc looked from one to the other.

“Where did you people come from?”

“He found me,” Pixel said.

“Dug me out,” Beard added.

Doc looked at Ghost.

“And who found you?”

“Found myself.”

“I can see that.”

He looked again at the dead man.

“We need to take him.”

Beard stopped smiling.

Ghost shook his head.

“The four of us can’t carry him. One’s got a gunshot leg. One was just under a collapsed roof. You’ve got one arm.”

“I know.”

“Then come on.”

Doc did not move.

“He’s from my company.”

Ghost squatted in front of him.

“They’ll recover him.”

“When?”

“I don’t know.”

“Exactly.”

Pixel stayed quiet. Even Beard did not cut in.

Doc rubbed one palm over his knee.

“What do I tell his mother?”

Ghost looked at him.

“The truth.”

“What truth?”

“That you were with him to the end.”

Doc pressed his lips together.

“That’ll help a lot.”

“I don’t know.”

“Then don’t talk bullshit.”

“Fine.”

Ghost stood.

“But if you stay here, somebody will have to explain things to two mothers.”

Doc looked sharply at him.

“My parents are dead.”

“Then somebody else.”

For a few seconds they stared at one another.

Then Doc exhaled.

“Give me your pressure dressing.”

“What?”

“You got an individual dressing pack?”

Ghost checked his med kit.

“Yeah.”

“Give it. Used mine already.”

It took two of them to redo Doc’s arm. He told Ghost what to hold, where to press, and when not to stick his fingers anywhere. He did his own swearing too.

“Tighter.”

Ghost pulled.

“Like that?”

“More.”

“That’s going to hurt.”

“Thank you so fucking much for the warning.”

Beard stood above them watching.

“Nice to see a doctor being treated by a patient.”

Doc looked up at him.

“You’re next.”

“I’m fine.”

“You walk sideways like a crab.”

“That’s style.”

“Tell them at the stab point.”

Pixel had meanwhile sat on the edge of the crater and stretched out his leg.

“Doc.”

“What?”

“What do you really think?”

“About what?”

“The leg.”

“What I really think is that if you ask me again, I’ll finish you off myself.”

“Got it.”

“Nobody’s giving you a prognosis here. Bleeding’s stopped—that’s what matters. Now get there.”

Pixel nodded.

“I’ve heard that somewhere already.”

Ghost did not answer.

When the dressing was done, Doc checked his fingers, clenched and opened them several times.

“Can live with it.”

“Get up,” Ghost said.

“You say that to everybody?”

“So far it works.”

Doc got up by himself. Swayed a little, but stayed upright.

Then he bent down toward the dead man. Said something quietly. So quietly Ghost could not make it out.

Doc straightened.

“Let’s go.”

He took his rifle in his left hand. Tried to sling the medical pack over his shoulder, but the right arm refused immediately.

Ghost took the pack from him.

“I can carry it.”

“I can see.”

“That’s fifteen kilos.”

“Then it’s light.”

Doc looked at him.

“What’s wrong with you?”

“Got scratched.”

“Where?”

“Side.”

“Show me.”

“At the stab point.”

Doc narrowed his eyes.

“I’m a medic, in case you missed it.”

“Noticed. You’re also a patient.”

“Doesn’t stop me.”

“It stops me.”

Doc was about to say something, but Beard shouted from above:

“You two getting married down there or are we leaving?”

“Leaving,” Ghost answered.

“Shame,” Pixel said. “Cute couple.”

It was ten meters from the crater to the tree line. Ghost went first, then Doc. Beard and Pixel brought up the rear.

Inside the trees, all four dropped to the ground almost at once.

“Hundred meters,” Pixel said.

“Ten,” Ghost answered.

“I automatically multiply now.”

Doc sat with his back against a trunk and studied Pixel.

“Give me the leg.”

“What for?”

“Just want to touch it. I’m a pervert.”

“Oh. Sure, then.”

Without unwrapping anything, Doc checked the dressing and the tourniquet.

“We need a time on this.”

“I don’t know the exact time.”

“We write when it was tightened. About 13:06.”

Ghost looked at his watch.

12:17.

Still not moving.

Doc noticed.

“What time have you got?”

Ghost showed him.

“Wow.”

“Dead.”

“The watch?”

“Yeah.”

“Good.”

Ghost looked at him.

Doc was quiet for a second, then smiled with one corner of his mouth.

“I meant the watch.”

“Oh.”

“You’re kind of jumpy.”

“Is that a medical examination?”

“Already started.”

Doc pulled a marker from a medical pouch and wrote on Pixel’s tourniquet.

“Now at least we won’t be making things up.”

Pixel looked at the writing.

“Pretty.”

“I spent six years in medical school specifically for this.”

Beard turned his head.

“You really a doctor?”

“Yeah. Worked ambulance.”

“Then why Doc?”

“Because ‘Doctor’ sounds like a shitty call sign.”

“And Doc is good?”

“I didn’t choose it. Some idiot in the company said I looked like a doctor from a TV show.”

Pixel perked up.

“Which one?”

“Fuck knows. I didn’t watch the show.”

“Maybe House?”

Doc looked at his arm.

“Maybe I look more like House now.”

“He limped.”

“Then you’re House.”

“No,” Pixel said. “I’m Pixel.”

Beard closed his eyes.

“Fuck, what a powerful conversation.”

For a few seconds it was quiet.

Then Doc asked:

“Anybody got signal?”

Pixel checked his phone.

“Zero.”

Beard shook his head.

Ghost did not even bother taking his out.

Doc ran a finger over the pocket where his phone should have been, then remembered.

“Right. Lost mine somewhere.”

“Wife gonna chew you out?” Beard asked.

“Daughter.”

“Grown?”

“Seventeen.”

“Oh. Then she definitely will.”

Doc nodded.

“She bought it for me.”

Pixel looked up.

“By herself?”

“Worked part-time in a coffee shop. Saved up. Said, ‘Dad, your phone makes you look homeless.’”

“What did you have?”

“A normal one.”

“What kind?”

“Buttons.”

Pixel grimaced as if he had just heard about a particularly serious crime.

“Doc, that’s not normal.”

“It made calls.”

“And?”

“What else is a phone supposed to do?”

Pixel opened his mouth, but Beard waved him off.

“Don’t start. We’ll never reach the stab point.”

Doc smiled.

“She wants to go to medical school.”

“Your daughter?” Beard asked.

“Yeah.”

“Like her dad.”

“Exactly why I tell her not to.”

“And she?”

“Tells me to get lost.”

Pixel nodded.

“Smart.”

“Very. Says, ‘You went.’”

Doc looked at his bandaged hand.

“And what the fuck can you say to that?”

Nobody answered for him.

A short burst of gunfire cracked somewhere to the left.

Ghost lifted his head.

“Break’s over.”

Pixel groaned.

“I just started.”

“You’ll rest farther on.”

“Where?”

“In a hundred meters.”

Beard laughed.

“Now there are four of us who know that’s a lie.”

Doc tried to get up, pushing against the trunk with his left hand.

Ghost held out a hand.

“I can do it.”

“You said that already.”

Doc took it.

Stood.

Pixel looked at them from below.

“Doc.”

“What?”

“If we make it, will you actually check my leg properly?”

“If we make it, there’ll be people smarter than me there.”

“And you?”

“I’ll tell them you pissed everybody off all the way here. Important medical history.”

Pixel smiled and started getting up.

Beard immediately put a shoulder under him.

Doc looked at them.

“He carrying you?”

“No,” Pixel said. “This is just how we’re friends.”

“Right.”

Ghost shouldered Doc’s medical pack along with his own. His side answered with a stab of pain. He had to freeze for a second.

Doc saw it.

“Scratched, huh?”

“Yeah.”

“I’ll catch you eventually.”

“Catch up first.”

“Fine. Four witnesses now that you asked for it.”

They moved along the tree line.

Doc walked second. For the first few minutes he was quiet, then began looking back.

Once.

Twice.

The third time Ghost said:

“Don’t look.”

“Don’t order me around.”

“Fine.”

Doc looked back again.

Then he stopped.

A few dozen meters later he caught up with Ghost.

“Ghost.”

“What?”

“I memorized the coordinates.”

“Good.”

“I’ll pass them on.”

“Good.”

Doc walked beside him a few more steps.

“His name was Serhii.”

Ghost looked at him.

“Good.”

Doc nodded.

And dropped one step behind.

Ahead the tree line bent. Beyond the bend the ground was black from a fresh impact. Several trees lay across the path.

Ghost climbed over the first one.

Beard and Pixel started around it.

Doc suddenly stopped.

“Quiet.”

Everybody froze.

Nothing came from under the fallen trees.

Then, very faintly:

“Hey…”

Pixel closed his eyes.

“Fuck.”

Beard looked at Ghost.

“Don’t even think about your hundred meters.”

Ghost was already taking off the medical pack.

Doc shifted his rifle into his left hand.

“Where?”

The voice came again. From under the earth.

“Here… fuck… here…”

Ghost pointed at the pile.

“There.”

Doc looked at his bandaged arm, then at the debris.

“Well, rescuer?”

Ghost crouched beside the first log.

“You’re the medic.”

Doc stepped up beside him.

“Fuck you. One.”
