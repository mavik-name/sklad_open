EVACUATION, OR ON THE ROAD TO THE STABILIZATION POINT
Viktor Makarchuk · MaVik

Story Five

Cashier

They did not cross the road right away.

Ghost raised a fist and everybody stopped at the last trees. About thirty meters ahead, a battered dirt road ran across their path. To the right, in the ditch, an overturned pickup lay on its roof. The cab had burned out; one wheel still stuck into the air.

“That your hundred and fifty?” Pixel asked quietly.

“Less now.”

“Fucking excellent. Geometry of victory.”

Beard crouched and leaned his back against a tree.

“Give me a minute.”

Nobody argued.

Shorty sat down too. He stretched the leg in front of him and stared at it as if it belonged to somebody else and was behaving indecently.

Doc came over.

“Don’t touch it,” Shorty said.

“I haven’t done anything yet.”

“Keep it that way.”

Doc sat beside him.

Ghost watched the road.

That was when they heard:

“Hey!”

Everybody froze.

“Hey, fuck!”

Pixel turned his head.

“Wasn’t me.”

“Thank you,” Beard said. “We thought you’d learned how to talk through roads.”

The voice came from below. Somewhere under the embankment.

Ghost climbed into the ditch. A concrete culvert stuck out under the road, half clogged with dirt and trash. It was dark inside.

“Where are you?”

“Here.”

“I can see that.”

“Then why the fuck are you asking?”

Ghost bent down.

Several meters in sat a man in body armor. His helmet lay beside him. His left arm was wrapped in some piece of cloth, and blood darkened one trouser leg.

“Can you crawl out?”

“I can.”

“Then crawl out.”

“No.”

Ghost waited.

“Why?”

“Something’s flying up there.”

“What?”

“Fuck knows. Buzzes. I stuck my head out twice already. Didn’t like it.”

Beard and Doc came down. Pixel stayed above with Shorty.

Doc peered into the pipe.

“You wounded?”

“No, I’m taking my vacation in here.”

“That arm yours?”

“Was this morning.”

“The leg?”

“Came in the same package.”

“Medic coming toward you.”

“Don’t.”

“Need to.”

“I said don’t. Two guys already crawled in here.”

Doc stopped.

“Where?”

The man nodded into the darkness behind him.

At first Ghost saw nothing.

Then his eyes adjusted.

Two men were lying farther inside the pipe.

Doc crawled in without a word.

The man did not look back.

“I told them to get in here. Pickup got hit, I yelled for the pipe. They came after me. One crawled himself. I dragged the other. Thought we’d wait it out.”

He rubbed his face with one palm.

“We waited it out.”

Nobody said anything.

Doc was not in there long. He came back just as silently and sat by the entrance.

“How long?”

“Forty minutes. Maybe an hour. I don’t know.”

“Tourniquet on the arm?”

“No. Wasn’t spraying.”

“Show me.”

“Go ahead.”

Doc unwound the cloth.

“Who did this?”

“Me.”

“Shows.”

“What are you, a fucking aesthete?”

“Today I am.”

Doc began dressing it properly.

Ghost squatted beside the pipe.

“Call sign?”

The man looked at him.

“Cashier.”

Beard snorted.

“Seriously?”

“What?”

“Nothing. We’ve already got Pixel, Shorty, Doc, Ghost. Now Cashier. All we need is Batman.”

Pixel called from above:

“I’m against it. Batman would get sick of us.”

Cashier smiled for the first time.

“How many of you?”

Ghost answered:

“Five.”

Cashier immediately counted the men he could see.

“Four.”

“Shorty’s up top.”

“Oh.”

He nodded.

“Then five.”

Doc tightened the dressing.

“Show me the leg.”

“It’s minor.”

“I’ll decide.”

“Oh, another boss.”

“No. You’re just bad at deciding.”

There were several fragment cuts on the thigh. One was deeper, but the bleeding had almost stopped.

Doc cleaned what he could and covered it.

“You can stand.”

Cashier looked at him.

“I can stand anyway.”

“Great. Then stand.”

“No.”

Beard finally lost patience.

“You’re pissing me off. Why ‘no’?”

Cashier nodded upward.

“I told you. It’s flying up there.”

“We were up there too.”

“Good for you.”

“And we got here.”

“Even better for you.”

Ghost asked:

“Who are you planning to wait for?”

Cashier did not answer.

Then:

“Our guys.”

“Which guys?”

“Evac.”

“You called them?”

“Radio’s dead. No cell network.”

“Then how do they know you’re here?”

Cashier looked at him angrily.

“They’ll know.”

Ghost said nothing.

Cashier held his gaze another ten seconds.

Then looked away.

“They know the route.”

“Seen the pickup?”

“Yeah.”

“Will a second one drive in here?”

“No.”

“How do you know?”

“I don’t.”

“Then don’t bullshit me.”

Ghost nodded.

“Fine.”

Cashier smiled again. For one second.

Then looked back into the pipe.

“There were three of us.”

Nobody answered.

“Vanya driving. Gray beside him. Me in the back. First strike fuck knows where. Second hit the vehicle. I either got thrown out or climbed out—I don’t remember. Yelled for them to come here. Vanya was still swearing. Gray wasn’t saying anything.”

Cashier rubbed the bridge of his nose.

“I keep counting. Three. Then two. Then one.”

Beard crouched opposite him.

“Why Cashier?”

Cashier looked at him as if he had not understood the question.

“Call sign.”

“Oh. Worked at a currency exchange before the war.”

Pixel heard from above.

“Actual cashier?”

“Actual.”

“Changed money?”

“No, fuck, I stroked dollar bills.”

“You know the rate?”

“Your rate right now is one way: the fuck out of here.”

Pixel laughed.

Cashier rubbed a hand over his face.

“Sat there ten years. Wife used to say I should’ve picked myself a decent call sign. Told her it was too late—it stuck.”

“What did she want?” Shorty asked.

“‘Handsome.’”

Beard snorted.

“Liar.”

“One hundred percent. She’s never called me that in her life.”

Ghost stood.

“Cashier.”

“What?”

“There are six of us now.”

Cashier looked at him.

“I’m not going anywhere.”

“I didn’t ask.”

“Are you deaf?”

“No.”

“Then you heard me.”

Ghost nodded toward the two men in the darkness.

“You can’t bring them out anymore.”

Cashier snapped his head up.

Beard said quietly:

“Ghost.”

“What?”

“Nothing.”

Cashier stared at Ghost for a long time.

“You think I don’t know?”

“You know.”

“Then what the fuck?”

“Because you’re sitting here with them.”

Cashier opened his mouth but said nothing.

Ghost turned toward the exit.

“We’re leaving.”

“Go.”

“Fine.”

He climbed out of the pipe.

Beard followed him.

Doc stayed another minute.

“You really leaving him?” he asked above.

Ghost looked at the road.

“No.”

“Then what are you doing?”

“Waiting.”

A minute later swearing came from the pipe.

Then a helmet appeared.

Then Cashier.

He crawled out by himself, sat in the ditch, and breathed heavily.

“I hate psychologists.”

Ghost held out a hand.

“I’m not a psychologist.”

“Even worse.”

Cashier stood.

He favored the leg, but he was steady.

“Where?”

Ghost pointed at the tree line across the road.

“There.”

Cashier looked at the open thirty meters.

Then he saw the pickup.

It lay about twenty meters to the right. Black, upside down. From where they stood, the charred driver’s seat was visible.

Cashier stopped joking.

“I already ran over there,” he said. “After the first hit. Thought I’d get Vanya out. Then the second one came.”

Ghost stepped closer.

“Look at me.”

“Why?”

“Not the vehicle. Me.”

Cashier shifted his eyes.

“And?”

“We go.”

“Where?”

“Across the road.”

“You’re very fucking specific.”

“I know.”

Ghost stepped onto the road.

Cashier did not follow.

Ghost took another step.

“One.”

Cashier frowned.

“What?”

“Two.”

“What are you doing?”

“Three.”

Pixel said quietly to Beard:

“He broke.”

“Don’t interfere.”

Ghost took a fourth step.

“Four.”

Cashier stayed where he was.

“Five.”

“Ghost, come back.”

“Six.”

“Oh for fuck’s sake.”

Cashier stepped after him.

“Seven,” Ghost said.

“That’s my first.”

“No. My seventh.”

“That’s cheating.”

“Eight.”

They walked.

Cashier looked only at Ghost’s back.

“Nine.”

“Count faster.”

“Ten.”

“I said faster.”

“Eleven.”

“Asshole.”

Halfway across, Cashier lost his step.

Ghost stopped.

“Don’t stop!”

“You stopped too.”

“Because you did.”

“Then walk.”

“Twelve.”

Cashier moved again.

The others were already behind them. Beard with Shorty, Doc beside them, Pixel last.

“Thirteen.”

“Fourteen,” Cashier said.

Ghost glanced over his shoulder.

“Fifteen.”

“Sixteen.”

“Seventeen.”

When they reached the trees, Cashier said on his own:

“Twenty-three.”

Ghost stopped.

“Twenty-two.”

“No.”

“Twenty-two.”

“I’m a cashier. Don’t bullshit me about numbers.”

Beard sat heavily under a tree.

“Oh. We found the man who can beat him.”

Pixel dropped beside him.

“I’m with Cashier.”

Shorty leaned against the trunk.

“Me too.”

Doc checked the dressing on Cashier’s arm.

“Not bleeding.”

“Told you.”

“You’ve told us a lot of things.”

Cashier looked back at the road.

The pickup was almost hidden from here.

He stood there a few seconds.

Then began counting.

Quietly, on his fingers.

“One. Two. Three. Four. Five.”

Pointed at himself.

“Six.”

Pixel asked:

“What are you doing?”

Cashier lowered his hand.

“Balancing the till.”

“And?”

He looked at the road one more time.

“Balances.”

Ghost was already moving farther along the tree line.

Cashier noticed.

“Hey!”

Ghost turned.

“How far to the stab point?”

Pixel groaned.

“Don’t ask him.”

Ghost thought.

“A hundred meters.”

Cashier looked at the rest.

“He always like this?”

Beard got up.

“Worse.”

“Then we’re fine.”

Cashier adjusted his rifle and followed them.
