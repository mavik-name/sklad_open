EVACUATION, OR ON THE ROAD TO THE STABILIZATION POINT
Viktor Makarchuk · MaVik

Story One

Pixel

Getting up did not happen on the first try.

Ghost planted a palm against the ground, pulled one knee under himself, and sat up. His head was ringing. Not so badly that he could hear nothing at all, but every sound came through a thin whine, as if somebody nearby had left an old television switched on.

“Fuck.”

The television did not switch off.

He sat there a little longer. The world swam in front of his eyes, then settled. Ten meters ahead, the trench ended in a fresh collapse. Where there had been overhead cover that morning, boards, sandbags, and a strip of black plastic now stuck out of the earth. Smoke drifted slowly above the tree line.

Ghost ran a hand over his helmet. Still there. His rifle was nearby too, half buried in dirt. He pulled it free and shook clods off the magazine.

Then he checked himself.

Left arm works. Right one too. Both legs. Fingers move. Something burned under the left side of his armor, especially when he twisted, but he could breathe. There was blood on his sleeve. A little on the glove. Whose—fuck knows.

“Good enough.”

He said it aloud because there was nobody else to say anything to.

The radio was dead. He stabbed at the button, changed channels, keyed the mic again.

“Fox, this is Ghost. How copy?”

Silence.

“Fox, Ghost. Come in.”

Nothing.

The antenna was intact. So was the casing. Ghost pulled the battery, pushed it back in. No help.

“Fucking perfect.”

His watch had stopped too. 12:17. The glass was not cracked, but the digits were not moving. He tapped the screen with one finger as if that might change something.

“That’s it. The gear’s decided it’s had enough.”

The phone in his chest pocket was even better: the screen was a spiderweb and it did not react to the button.

The stabilization point was about five kilometers away in a straight line. Nobody went in a straight line. Through friendly positions, down the ravine, and along the old dirt road it was farther. How much farther did not matter right now. What mattered was that he was on his feet.

And if you’re on your feet, you walk.

Ghost slung the rifle, tightened the strap of his plate carrier, and climbed through the collapse where a passage was still open.

The first hundred meters were fine. Then his side began to pull. Not sharply. More as if somebody had shoved a hot stone under his armor and it was slowly pressing into his ribs.

“They’ll have a look at it at the stab point,” he muttered.

He was moving along what remained of the trench when he heard:

“Fuuuck…”

Ghost stopped.

Silence.

Then again, quieter this time:

“Fuck… fuck… fuck…”

“Very informative,” Ghost said. “Where are you?”

The swearing stopped.

“Who’s there?”

“Santa Claus. Where the fuck are you?”

“Here.”

“Where the fuck is ‘here’?”

“Right here!”

Ghost closed his eyes.

“Sure, I’ll find you on the map by voice. Keep yelling.”

To the right of the trench was a shallow pit, once probably an observation post. One edge had collapsed after the strike. A combat boot stuck out from behind a mound of earth.

Ghost climbed over the parapet.

The soldier lay on his side, wedged between the wall of the pit and a crate that had once held some kind of drone gear. He looked about twenty-five. His glasses had somehow survived, though his helmet had not. The right trouser leg above the knee was dark with blood. A tourniquet was already high on the thigh, but blood was still soaking the fabric below it.

The guy saw Ghost and tried to smile.

“Oh. A human being.”

“Debatable. Why are you lying around here?”

“Resting.”

“I can see that. Five-star resort, fucker.”

Ghost sat beside him.

“Call sign?”

“Pixel.”

“Where does it hurt, Pixel?”

“Nowhere. I just like my pants wet.”

“So you can talk. That’s something.”

Ghost looked at the tourniquet.

“Put it on yourself?”

“Yeah.”

“You did a shit job.”

“Thank you, doctor.”

“I’m not a doctor.”

“Then fuck off.”

“I’ll tighten this first. Then you can fuck off yourself.”

Pixel got as far as “don’t,” and after that he swore without pauses while Ghost cranked the tourniquet down until the bleeding finally stopped.

“Aaah, fuck, fuck! Fucking hell! You said you weren’t a doctor!”

“Exactly. That’s why there’s no anesthesia.”

Pixel breathed hard, fingers clawed into the dirt.

“They gonna cut the leg off?”

“Who?”

“The doctors.”

“Get to the doctors first. Then negotiate what they’re going to cut off.”

“I’m serious.”

“So am I.”

Ghost ran a hand down the leg, checking for anything else obvious. The boot was intact. Pixel moved his foot and immediately grimaced.

“Don’t move it.”

“I was checking.”

“What?”

“Whether the leg’s still there.”

“It is.”

“I can’t feel it.”

“Because of the tourniquet, genius.”

“Oh.”

Pixel lay quietly for a moment.

“Then that’s good.”

Ghost found his helmet a few meters away and brought it back. Then he pulled a small pack out from under the dirt. Something clinked inside.

“Don’t break my stuff,” Pixel said.

“What’s in there?”

“A Mavic.”

Ghost looked at him.

“They might be putting you in a body bag in a minute and you’re worried about a drone?”

“It belongs to the state.”

“And you’re privately owned?”

“My mother made me. Not the state.”

“Fair point.”

Ghost set the pack beside him.

“Got water?”

Pixel nodded toward a pouch. Ghost took out the bottle, unscrewed the cap, and handed it over.

“Slowly.”

Pixel took a few sips.

“You?”

“I’ve got my own.”

Ghost had not, in fact, found his water. The pouch had probably been torn off. But he did not feel like taking water from a wounded man.

Pixel drank a little more and gave the bottle back.

“Where’d you come from?”

Ghost waved behind him.

“Back there.”

“Everything got smashed back there.”

“I noticed.”

“See any of ours?”

“No.”

“Me neither. Crawled out after the first hit, wanted to get to the next dugout. Then another one landed. Next thing I remember, I’m already here. Put this thing on and stayed down.”

He touched the tourniquet and immediately pulled his hand away.

“What time is it?”

Ghost automatically looked at his watch.

12:17.

“Watch is dead.”

“I’ve got my phone.”

The phone was under Pixel. Ghost helped him pull it out. The screen lit up.

13:06.

He looked at his own watch again.

“That hit really did a number on me.”

“What?”

“Nothing.”

Pixel unlocked the phone. No signal.

“Three percent.”

“Save it. You’ll find a power bank at the stab point.”

“You think we’ll make it?”

Ghost looked at him.

There was no joke in the question.

Pixel was no longer looking at his leg. He stared off to one side, at the shattered parapet. His face had gone gray. Maybe from the blood loss, maybe because he had finally stopped swearing and started thinking.

“Can you walk by yourself?” Ghost asked.

“On one leg?”

“On two. Just don’t use one of them much.”

“You saw what happened to it?”

“I did.”

“And?”

“And lying here is a shitty plan.”

Pixel snorted.

“You got a better one?”

“Yeah. Stand up.”

“Brilliant.”

“Then walk.”

“Even more brilliant.”

“See? Two steps already.”

Pixel closed his eyes.

“I can’t.”

Ghost said nothing.

Something boomed far away. Not toward them. Then again. A familiar drone buzz passed over the tree line, high up, and quickly faded.

“Pixel.”

“Fuck off.”

“Fine.”

Ghost stood.

Pixel opened his eyes.

“Where are you going?”

“You told me to fuck off.”

“Oh, for fuck’s sake…”

Ghost picked up his pack.

“I’ll take the Mavic. Government property. Shame to let it go to waste.”

“Put it down, asshole.”

“No. Good drone.”

“There’s a memory card in there too.”

“All the more reason.”

Pixel propped himself up on an elbow.

“You’re fucked in the head.”

“I’ve been told.”

“Help me sit up.”

Ghost came back.

Sitting up turned out to be easier than standing. When Pixel tried to put weight on his right leg, pain folded him so hard he nearly fell backward.

“Easy. Don’t play hero.”

“You’re telling me that?”

“At least I’m not lying in a hole.”

“Yet.”

Ghost hooked Pixel’s arm over his shoulder.

“On three.”

“I hate this ‘on three’ shit.”

“One.”

“Wait.”

“Two.”

“I said wait, fuck!”

“Three.”

Pixel yelled. Not loudly—more in anger than anything. But he stood.

For a few seconds they simply stayed there. Pixel hung on Ghost with almost all his weight, breathing fast straight into his ear.

“Well?”

“What do you mean, ‘well’?”

“You’re standing.”

“Fuck you.”

“Later. Step now.”

The first step was nothing. The second was worse. On the third, Pixel found a way to put down his right foot without really loading it and stopped hanging so heavily on Ghost’s shoulder.

It took them about five minutes to reach the trench, though it was only twenty meters away.

Pixel lowered himself onto the edge and said nothing for a long time.

“I’m not walking five kilometers.”

“You don’t have to walk five.”

“How far, then?”

“To the next place where we sit down.”

“And where’s that?”

Ghost pointed about a hundred meters ahead, where the trench disappeared around a bend.

“There.”

“And then?”

“Then another hundred.”

Pixel looked at him.

“You a psychologist or something?”

“No.”

“Not a medic. Not a psychologist. What the fuck are you, then?”

“Ghost.”

“I heard that part.”

Pixel wiped sweat from his forehead.

“Pixel because of drones?”

“Because of service work.”

“What kind of service?”

“Fixed phones. Before the war. Screens, batteries, charging ports. Then somehow I went from iPhones to Chinese quadcopters bought with government money.”

“Career growth.”

“Tell me about it. Mom still thinks I’m sitting at some headquarters setting up computers.”

“How long has she thought that?”

“Second year now.”

“Smart woman.”

“She’ll kill me if she finds out.”

Ghost nodded at the leg.

“Then you definitely can’t die here. She’ll be pissed somebody beat her to it.”

Pixel laughed once and immediately winced.

“Fuck… Don’t make me laugh. Everything hurts.”

“Good.”

“What’s good?”

“That it hurts. Gives you something to do on the way.”

“You really are fucked in the head.”

“Get up.”

“Already?”

“Already.”

This time Pixel did not say he couldn’t.

They moved.

The trench was less badly damaged farther on. In places they had to climb over fallen earth; in others they walked along the top, crouching low. Ghost carried both packs and kept a hand under Pixel’s elbow. Every few dozen meters Pixel stopped.

He did not ask to stop. He simply did.

Ghost stopped too.

The burning in his side was getting worse. He tried not to twist or bend sharply. Pixel asked him twice what was wrong. Both times Ghost answered:

“Got scratched.”

The third time, Pixel did not ask.

Around the bend they found an abandoned entrenching tool, one glove, and half a crate of field rations scattered through the dirt. Pixel spotted the crackers.

“Oh. Lunch.”

“Don’t.”

“Why?”

“Because then you’ll need to shit.”

“Fair enough.”

About fifty meters later he suddenly stopped and started feeling around a pocket in his plate carrier.

“What’d you lose?”

“My phone.”

“It’s in your hand.”

Pixel looked at the phone.

“Right.”

Ghost said nothing.

Neither did he.

A minute later Pixel spoke on his own.

“I promised Lera I’d text this morning.”

“You will.”

“We had a fight.”

“All the more reason.”

“I told her she was getting on my fucking nerves.”

“Romantic.”

“She said I was an idiot.”

“Maybe she’s just observant.”

“Fuck you.”

He was quiet for a bit.

“We were gonna get married on the twenty-seventh.”

“So get married.”

“What if they cut off my leg?”

Ghost turned his head.

“Registry office doesn’t let you in without a leg?”

Pixel stared at him for a few seconds, then snorted.

“You got an answer for everything?”

“No. You just keep asking stupid shit.”

“She bought the dress.”

“Now that’s a problem.”

“Why?”

“If you don’t show up, the money’s wasted.”

Pixel laughed again. Longer this time.

“Fuck, Ghost…”

“What?”

“Nothing.”

He put the phone in his pocket.

And walked on his own.

Not completely on his own: Ghost stayed beside him, ready to catch him. But Pixel was no longer hanging from his shoulder. He planted the good leg, dragged the wounded one forward, swore on every other step, and moved.

Slowly, but he moved.

When they reached a fork in the trench, Ghost stopped.

Left led toward the old tree line. Right led toward the ravine and farther back, toward the rear.

“We go right,” he said.

“You sure?”

“Sure.”

“Because you already said the next stop was a hundred meters. That was a hundred and seventy.”

“You counted?”

“I suffered. When you suffer, you feel every meter.”

Ghost adjusted the strap on Pixel’s pack.

“Next stop in a hundred.”

“Fuck you.”

“Come on.”

They turned right.

A few minutes later Pixel suddenly raised a hand.

“Stop.”

Ghost stopped.

“You hear that?”

At first all he heard was the same whine in his head. Weaker now, but still there.

Then something clanged ahead, beyond the embankment.

And a voice:

“Anybody alive?!”

Pixel looked at Ghost.

“Maybe we don’t go?”

“Why?”

“Just checking.”

Ghost took the rifle off his shoulder.

“Stay here.”

“Yeah, right. You’re the one who said lying around is a shitty plan.”

Ghost looked at him.

Pixel was already tightening the strap of his pack.

“Well?” he said. “Why’d you stop?”

And limping, he headed for the embankment first.
