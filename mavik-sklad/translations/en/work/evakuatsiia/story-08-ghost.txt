EVACUATION, OR ON THE ROAD TO THE STABILIZATION POINT
Viktor Makarchuk · MaVik

Story Eight

Ghost

Three hundred meters remained to the blocks.

After everything they had been through, those three hundred looked almost insulting.

A straight field road. No red signs. No craters where you had to guess where the old passage had been. Beyond the concrete blocks they could already see camouflage netting, several vehicles, and people in uniform.

Ours.

Pixel walked holding onto Beard. Beard swore quietly under his breath. Philosopher hung on Doc. Mongol carried Shorty almost like a wardrobe, except the wardrobe periodically demanded to be put down. Cashier walked in the middle and was not counting anybody anymore.

Ghost walked first.

His side did not burn anymore.

Strange. An hour ago every twist of his torso had sent pain under the plate carrier hard enough to make him want to stop. Now it only pulled. Maybe he had gotten used to it.

He even thought Doc had been bugging him for nothing.

“Hey!” somebody shouted from the blocks. “Stop!”

Ghost stopped.

Raised a hand.

“Friendlies!”

The soldier by the concrete block looked in their direction. Then waved to somebody behind him.

“Wounded?”

“Yeah!” Ghost shouted.

The soldier did not answer.

Cashier, standing about five meters behind him, barked:

“We’ve got wounded!”

The soldier immediately raised a hand.

“Come in! One at a time! Weapons slung!”

Ghost looked back at Cashier.

“I said it.”

“Maybe your voice isn’t listening to you anymore.”

“Yours works for two.”

“Professional.”

They moved on.

Two men stood by the blocks. One took a quick look over the group and shouted back:

“Seven wounded! Get stretchers over here!”

Cashier automatically raised a finger.

“Eight.”

The soldier counted again.

“Seven.”

“Eight,” Cashier repeated.

“Where’s the eighth?”

Cashier pointed at Ghost.

“This one.”

The soldier looked where he was pointing.

Ghost stood two meters away.

“Which one?”

Cashier frowned.

“Ghost.”

“Who?”

“For fuck’s sake…”

Beard tugged at his sleeve.

“Don’t start. Let us get in.”

“But he…”

“Later.”

They were already being taken in hand.

Pixel was put on a stretcher almost by force.

“I can do it myself.”

“Sit.”

“I can walk.”

“We can see that.”

“I’m serious.”

“Kid, you’ve had a tourniquet on for three hours. Shut your mouth.”

Pixel looked at Ghost.

“See? Same kind of people here too.”

Ghost smiled.

“Means they’re ours.”

The medic standing nearby did not even turn his head.

Ghost waved a hand.

“Tourniquet went on sometime around one. He put it on himself, I tightened it at thirteen oh-six. Hasn’t been loosened since.”

The medic was looking at Pixel.

“When did the tourniquet go on?”

Pixel blinked.

“He just told you.”

“Who?”

“Ghost.”

“Which Ghost?”

Pixel pointed.

“This one.”

The medic looked.

Then at Pixel.

“Did you hit your head?”

“No.”

“Lose consciousness?”

“Are you fucking serious?”

Doc, who was already being seated on a crate near the entrance, heard them.

“The tourniquet was on before 13:06. Ghost tightened it. I checked it later. It wasn’t released.”

The medic nodded.

“Now that’s information.”

Pixel stared at him.

“And what was wrong with his?”

“With whose?”

“Oh, fuck this.”

Ghost stood beside them.

At first it was almost funny.

After several hours of everybody talking at once, Mongol hearing only half of anything, Pixel arguing with everyone, Beard telling everybody to fuck off, Cashier counting people, and Shorty threatening to kill Mongol for helping him, the silence around Ghost felt almost like a rest.

He stepped aside.

The stab point was not how he had pictured it on the way: camouflage netting, two tents, a medical vehicle, several stretchers, boxes of water. Somewhere farther behind the trees, an engine was running.

Fine.

They had made it.

“Ghost!” Pixel shouted.

Ghost turned.

Pixel was already lying on a stretcher. Two medics were working on his leg.

“Where are you going?”

“Nowhere.”

“They need to look at you too.”

Doc called from his crate:

“There! Witness number two. Ghost, get over here.”

“In a second.”

“No. Not in a second. Here.”

Ghost took several steps toward him.

Doc looked straight at him.

“Take the plate carrier off.”

“Doc, fuck off.”

The medic beside Doc looked at him.

“Who are you talking to?”

Doc turned his head.

“Ghost.”

“Who?”

Doc smiled, not understanding yet.

“Thi—”

He pointed.

The smile disappeared.

The medic followed his finger.

“There’s nobody there.”

For several seconds Doc said nothing.

“Ghost.”

“What?” Ghost answered.

Doc looked at him.

He heard him.

Definitely heard him.

“Say something.”

“You’re fucking annoying.”

Doc slowly turned to the medic.

“He just said I’m fucking annoying.”

“Who?”

“Ghost.”

The medic stopped working on his arm.

“You get concussed?”

“No.”

“Lose consciousness?”

“No.”

“Then who are you seeing?”

Doc did not answer.

Beard heard the conversation and lifted his head.

“What’s going on?”

“He can’t see Ghost.”

“Who?”

“This guy.”

“Maybe he’s blind.”

The medic looked at Beard.

“Uncle, who exactly am I supposed to be seeing?”

“Ghost.”

“Where is he?”

“Right fucking there.”

Beard pointed.

Shorty, who had already been laid on another stretcher, pushed himself up on his elbows.

“Ghost, come over here. These guys are about to decide we’ve all lost our fucking minds.”

Ghost stood between them.

“I’m already here.”

Shorty looked at the medic.

“Hear that?”

The medic shook his head.

Mongol, sitting on the ground and still hearing the world through cotton, looked around at everybody.

“What?”

Cashier leaned toward him.

“THEY CAN’T SEE GHOST.”

Mongol frowned.

“Which Ghost?”

Everybody went silent.

Shorty even stopped breathing for a second.

Mongol looked at Ghost.

“Oh. This one.”

Pixel burst out laughing on the stretcher.

“Fuck. You had me worried there.”

Even Beard laughed.

For several seconds the tension let go.

Mongol added:

“Why can’t they see him?”

Cashier sat down right on the ground.

“And now it’s fucked again.”

Philosopher was still standing by the entrance, leaning on an orderly’s shoulder. The orderly was trying to pull him inside, but Philosopher would not go.

He was looking at Ghost.

“You led us through the field.”

“Yeah.”

“I can see you.”

“That’s good already.”

“Don’t joke.”

Ghost stopped smiling.

Suddenly there were a lot of people around them.

Medics. Orderlies. Somebody from security. Somebody with a radio. They were looking at seven wounded men who were all pointing at the same place.

Nobody was looking at him.

Not the way people look through a person.

There simply was nothing there for them.

Ghost touched his plate carrier.

“Doc.”

Doc answered immediately.

“I’m here.”

“You can see me?”

“Yes.”

“Pixel?”

“I can fucking see you.”

“Beard?”

“Ask me one more time and I’ll punch you in the face.”

“Shorty?”

“I see you.”

Cashier raised a hand.

“Six.”

Mongol raised his too.

“What six?”

“Nothing.”

Philosopher said:

“Seven.”

Cashier looked at him.

“Seven including me.”

“I mean the ones who can see him.”

Cashier slowly nodded.

The medic, the same one who had taken Pixel, was no longer asking about concussions.

“Call sign definitely Ghost?”

“Definitely,” Doc said.

“Unit?”

Doc looked at Ghost.

“I didn’t ask.”

Beard did too.

“Neither did I.”

Pixel lifted his head.

“He came from the positions back there. By the old trench. Everything got hit.”

“Name?”

Nobody answered.

The medic repeated:

“Anybody know his real name?”

Silence.

Ghost opened his mouth.

“Serhii.”

None of the medics moved.

Pixel immediately said:

“Serhii.”

Ghost looked at him.

Pixel was looking straight into his eyes.

“Serhii,” he repeated, quieter now. “He says Serhii.”

The medic picked up the radio.

“What unit?”

Ghost named it.

Pixel repeated it.

The medic called somebody over the radio. No answer the first time. He tried again.

Ghost stood staring at the little black speaker.

Static.

Then a voice.

“Go ahead.”

Ghost recognized it immediately.

Fox.

He stepped toward the radio.

“Fox.”

Nobody reacted.

The medic keyed the mic.

“You have a soldier, call sign Ghost? Serhii.”

A pause.

Static.

“Repeat.”

“Ghost. Serhii. We’ve got a group of wounded saying he brought them out to us.”

The pause was longer this time.

Ghost stared at the radio.

“Fox, it’s me.”

Silence.

Then:

“That can’t be.”

The medic looked at the seven men.

“Why?”

The radio did not answer right away.

“Ghost is KIA.”

Nobody said anything.

Not even Mongol.

The medic asked again:

“We talking about the same guy?”

“Serhii, call sign Ghost. Yes.”

Pixel sat up on the stretcher.

“No.”

The medic gestured for him to lie back down.

Pixel pushed his hand away.

“No, fuck that. He found me. He tightened my tourniquet. He brought me here.”

The radio crackled.

“Who’s that?”

The medic pressed the button.

“Wounded. Seven of them. They all say the same thing.”

Cashier corrected him automatically:

“Eight.”

The medic heard him but did not argue this time.

“When was he killed?” Doc asked.

The medic relayed the question.

The radio went quiet.

Ghost looked down at his wrist.

At the watch.

12:17.

The digits stood exactly where they had when he first sat up in the wrecked trench and tapped a finger against the glass.

12:17.

The radio clicked.

“The strike was at twelve seventeen. Direct hit. The dugout collapsed. We got to him after the follow-up shelling. He was already gone.”

Ghost did not look at the people.

He looked at the watch.

Pixel said quietly:

“I saw him at thirteen oh-six.”

Nobody answered.

“My phone said thirteen oh-six. He even said his watch had died.”

Beard sank lower on the crate.

“He dug me out after Pixel.”

Doc said:

“Then he got me.”

“Pulled me out from under a tree,” Shorty said.

Cashier was looking at Ghost.

“Got me out of the pipe.”

Mongol had missed half the conversation, but he understood enough.

“He turned me around. I was going back.”

Philosopher smiled a smile that was not cheerful at all.

“And he brought me through the mines.”

The medic held the radio in one hand and said nothing.

The speaker came alive again.

“What the fuck are you people talking about over there?”

Pixel snatched the radio out of the medic’s hand before he could react.

“Fox?”

“Who is this?”

“Pixel. I don’t know Ghost. I’m not from your unit. He found me today. I was lying in a hole with a tourniquet on. He tightened it and pulled me out. Then he picked up six more. We can all see him.”

“Pixel, give me the radio,” the medic said.

“In a second.”

Nothing came from the speaker for several seconds.

Then Fox asked:

“What was he wearing?”

Pixel looked at Ghost.

“Like everybody else, fuck. Plate carrier, helmet. Rifle. Broken phone. Blood on the left sleeve.”

Ghost automatically looked at his sleeve.

The blood was there.

The same blood.

“What else?” Fox asked.

Pixel faltered.

“How the fuck should I know? Drove everybody insane with his ‘hundred meters.’ Married. Has kids. Wouldn’t show Doc his own wound.”

Doc said quietly:

“Fuck.”

Somebody exhaled heavily over the radio.

“That’s him.”

Pixel gave the radio back to the medic.

Nobody asked anything else.

Ghost stood there looking at his watch.

12:17.

Then he lifted his head.

Doc was looking at him.

“Ghost.”

“What?”

“Show me your side.”

Ghost laughed.

“Are you serious?”

“Yes.”

“What the fuck for now?”

Doc laughed too.

Briefly. Then covered his face with his good hand.

“Fuck you.”

“Finally.”

Beard sat nearby staring at the ground.

“So you were dead this whole time.”

Ghost shrugged.

“Looks like it.”

“And I was leaning on you.”

“You were.”

“And you moved that tree.”

“I did.”

“Fucking impressive.”

Cashier said:

“I counted with him across the road.”

Pixel looked at him.

“That’s what worries you most right now?”

“I’m a cashier. Numbers worry me.”

Shorty lay on the stretcher looking at Ghost.

“Ghost.”

“What?”

“What the fuck?”

Ghost thought about it.

“I don’t know.”

Philosopher snorted quietly.

“Finally, a normal answer.”

Mongol leaned toward Shorty.

“What did he say?”

Shorty looked at Ghost.

Then at Mongol.

“Says he doesn’t know.”

“Oh.”

Mongol nodded.

“Logical.”

The medics got back to work.

They had things to do that they understood. Pixel was being prepared for transport. Shorty’s leg was being immobilized. Doc finally let somebody properly deal with his arm. Beard was told to lie down; he told everyone to fuck off and lay down. Philosopher argued about painkillers. Mongol kept asking when his hearing would come back.

Cashier sat on the ground and did not take his eyes off Ghost.

A few minutes later Cashier asked:

“Where are you going now?”

Ghost looked around.

“I don’t know.”

“You were headed to the stab point.”

Ghost looked at the tent.

“Made it.”

“Then sit down.”

“You giving me orders?”

“Yeah.”

“Not very good at it.”

“Learning.”

Pixel called from the stretcher:

“Ghost.”

“What now?”

“I’ll write to Lera.”

Ghost nodded.

“I know.”

“How?”

“Because that dress cost money.”

Pixel laughed hard enough that the medic snapped at him.

Beard raised a hand without getting up.

“I’ll finish the greenhouse.”

Ghost looked at him.

“What greenhouse?”

“Long story.”

“Then finish it.”

Shorty said:

“And I’m not calling my idiot a dwarf anymore.”

Pixel turned his head.

“He’s the one who calls you that.”

“Fuck. Right.”

Even the medic laughed, though he understood none of it.

Mongol looked around at everyone.

“Why are you laughing?”

“DOESN’T MATTER,” Shorty shouted.

“Don’t shout.”

And then all seven of them laughed.

Ghost did too.

The medics looked at them and did not ask questions.

Then a vehicle arrived.

Everything became motion. Some were loaded first, others left waiting. Stretchers moved back and forth. Somebody swore at the driver. Somebody was looking for paperwork.

The group came apart as quickly as it had once come together.

Pixel was carried out first.

As the stretcher passed Ghost, he raised a hand.

Ghost gave him five.

Pixel nodded as if that was enough.

Beard was led out second. He stopped beside Ghost.

“Well.”

“Well.”

Beard held out a hand.

Ghost shook it.

“Thank you,” Beard said.

“Go already.”

“Why don’t you go fuck yourself.”

“Later.”

Beard laughed.

“That ‘later’ of yours is really something…”

He did not finish.

He left.

Doc stayed the longest.

He stood in front of Ghost with one arm in a splint.

“I’m a doctor,” he said.

“I know.”

“And now I somehow have to explain this to myself.”

“Good luck.”

“Fuck you.”

“You said that already.”

Doc nodded.

Then, very quietly:

“I didn’t leave him.”

Ghost understood who he meant.

The man Doc had been sitting beside in the crater.

“You didn’t.”

“I thought I did.”

“Nah.”

Doc stood there another second.

Then left.

Mongol was carrying Shorty again.

“Put me down, fuck, at least now!”

“What?”

“NOTHING!”

Cashier walked behind them, counting.

“One. Two. Three. Four. Five. Six.”

Philosopher limped last.

Cashier looked back at him.

“Seven.”

Then he looked at Ghost.

One finger rose again.

“Eight.”

The medic beside him said:

“Who are you always counting?”

Cashier did not take his eyes off Ghost.

“My own.”

The medic looked as if he wanted to answer, then thought better of it.

Cashier went to the vehicle.

Philosopher lingered.

“Well, Ghost.”

“What?”

“Turns out your call sign was pretty lazy.”

“How?”

“No imagination.”

Ghost smiled.

“Go already, Philosopher.”

“I’m an electrician.”

“All the more reason.”

Philosopher took two steps, then turned back.

“Listen.”

“What?”

Philosopher nodded toward the vehicle, where the others were already sitting. Mongol was adjusting Shorty. Beard was saying something to Pixel. Doc was arguing with Cashier.

“See that?”

Ghost looked.

“I see.”

“Good.”

Philosopher left.

The vehicle did not move right away.

Cashier shouted something from inside. Ghost could not make it out.

Mongol definitely did not hear it.

Then the engine roared, the vehicle jerked, and drove away along the road between the trees.

Ghost remained by the blocks.

One of the medics passed very close by.

“Good,” Ghost said.

Nobody answered.

He looked at his watch.

12:17.

Then at the road they had come by.

Something boomed far away.

An ordinary sound.

Ghost stood there a little longer.

And walked on.
