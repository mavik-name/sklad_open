EVACUATION, OR ON THE ROAD TO THE STABILIZATION POINT
Viktor Makarchuk · MaVik

Story Seven

Philosopher

“So far the numbers still balance.”

Ghost did not answer. He was staring at the red signs.

He could see three of them. One on a tree, another lower down almost in the grass, a third farther out in the open. Between them ran a strip of trampled earth, but after the shelling everything there looked equally bad: craters, dry clods, broken branches.

Pixel came closer.

“So I take it going around through the woods would be too easy?”

Ghost shook his head.

“Mines there too.”

“And right?”

“Same.”

Cashier breathed out.

“I love places with options.”

Doc watched Ghost.

“You know the lane?”

“I did.”

“When?”

“Day before yesterday.”

Beard lifted his head.

“And absolutely nothing could have changed here in two days? Little things. Shelling, for example.”

“Could have.”

“That’s what I love about you. Always reassuring.”

Mongol looked from one face to another.

“What?”

Shorty pointed at the sign.

“MINES.”

Mongol narrowed his eyes.

“I can see.”

“THEN WHY ARE YOU ASKING?”

“I asked why you’re standing around.”

Shorty closed his eyes.

“Lord.”

Pixel said:

“Don’t appeal upward. Cashier warned us already.”

Ghost took Doc’s pack off his shoulder and set it beside a tree.

“We leave anything unnecessary here.”

Doc immediately frowned.

“No fucking way.”

“Not talking about yours.”

“Then what?”

Ghost pointed at the piece of crate Shorty had, for no obvious reason, been dragging for the last half kilometer.

Shorty hugged the board against himself.

“It’s a seat.”

“It’s a board.”

“It’s my seat.”

Beard said:

“Shorty, if we blow ourselves up now, sitting down won’t be relevant anymore.”

Shorty thought about it and threw the board away.

“Convinced me.”

Ghost looked ahead again.

“Single file. Step in the footprints. Nobody spreads out. Where I go, you go. If I stop, everybody stops.”

“And if you’re wrong?” Cashier asked.

“Then we’ll find out.”

“Fuck.”

“You asked.”

Ghost went first.

Beard behind him. Then Pixel, Doc, Cashier. Mongol took Shorty so he barely had to put any weight on the left leg and went last.

“I can do it myself,” Shorty said.

Mongol did not hear.

“Thank God for that,” Shorty added.

Nothing happened for the first ten meters.

That did not make it easier.

Ghost moved slowly. He watched his feet, then looked ahead, searching for what he remembered. There had once been white tape on stakes here. One stake was gone now, another lay in the grass, a third leaned at an angle.

He stopped.

Everybody froze behind him.

Pixel whispered:

“Don’t tell me you forgot.”

“I didn’t.”

“Then why are we standing?”

Ghost pointed at the crater ahead.

“That wasn’t there.”

Beard said quietly:

“Fucking wonderful news.”

Doc looked back.

The trees were already twenty meters behind. Not far. But nobody suggested going back.

Ghost skirted the crater on the left. One step. Another.

Behind him came:

“Stop.”

The voice was not one of theirs.

Ghost froze.

Pixel did too.

“Who said that?”

“Not me,” Beard answered.

“Not me,” Cashier said.

To the right, about thirty meters away, was an old trench, almost leveled with the ground. A hand rose above the edge.

“Don’t go farther left,” the voice said. “It’s bad there.”

Ghost turned his head.

“Who are you?”

“Who are you?”

“Tourists,” Beard said.

Silence from the trench.

“Then this is a shitty tour.”

Pixel smiled.

“One of ours.”

Ghost looked at the ground between them and the trench.

“Can you get out yourself?”

“I can.”

“Then get out.”

“Can’t.”

Beard laughed quietly.

“Another one.”

“What happened?” Doc shouted.

“Leg.”

Doc actually groaned.

“Why is it always legs with you people?”

Shorty called from the back:

“Because our hands are busy holding rifles.”

Mongol leaned down.

“What did he say?”

“NOTHING.”

“Don’t shout.”

The man in the trench raised a hand again.

“I’m serious. Don’t come straight at me. Everything got mixed up here after yesterday.”

Ghost watched him.

“How’d you get in there?”

“On my feet.”

“Very funny.”

“I’m not finding it that funny anymore either.”

Ghost looked at the remains of white tape, then at the trench.

“You know where the lane is?”

“I know about ten meters from here. Can’t see farther.”

“How did you come in?”

A pause.

“Not from this side.”

Cashier said:

“Beautiful. We’ve got two guides and neither knows the road.”

“One of them knows a hundred meters,” Pixel said.

“That’s not a road. That’s a diagnosis.”

Ghost crouched.

He thought he remembered now. Not the ground—the direction. The safe lane had led toward the old trench, then right along it, then from there out onto the open patch. The day before yesterday they had entered from the other side.

“You’re sitting on the lane,” he shouted.

“Could be.”

“Not could. The corridor runs there.”

“Then congratulations. I’m occupying it.”

“Call sign?”

“Philosopher.”

Pixel immediately turned to Cashier.

“No Batman.”

“Day’s not over yet.”

Ghost moved on.

“Ghost,” Doc said quietly.

“What?”

“You sure?”

Ghost looked at him.

“No.”

Doc nodded.

“Good. Just wanted to clarify.”

They went several more meters in silence.

Then Beard said:

“I’ve been thinking.”

“Don’t,” Cashier answered.

“No, seriously. If I get out of here, I’m not walking anywhere for two weeks.”

Pixel whispered:

“You’ll call a sapper to take you from the couch to the toilet.”

“I’ll piss in a bucket.”

“Romantic.”

“Quiet,” Ghost said.

And they went quiet.

Fifteen meters remained to the trench.

Philosopher was clearer now. He sat on the bottom with his back against the wall, rifle across his knees. His right trouser leg was cut open below the knee and bandaged over it. A torn dressing wrapper lay beside him.

“Still bleeding?” Doc called.

“Not anymore.”

“Use a tourniquet?”

“No.”

“Good.”

“Glad I passed the exam.”

Ghost took another step and suddenly stopped.

Not because of the ground.

Far across the field to the left came the brief crack of automatic fire.

Everybody ducked.

Mongol stayed standing.

Shorty tugged his sleeve.

“SIT.”

“What?”

“SIT DOWN, FUCK!”

Mongol crouched.

“You could’ve just said so.”

Philosopher looked toward the sound from the trench.

“Gray zone over there. They come through sometimes.”

Ghost crouched lower.

“Far?”

“Don’t know. I didn’t come here with a map.”

Cashier said:

“Finally, a normal person. Doesn’t know anything either.”

“At least he doesn’t bullshit us about a hundred meters,” Pixel added.

Ghost looked back.

“Want me to leave you here?”

“No,” Beard and Pixel said together.

Philosopher laughed.

“You been together long?”

Cashier answered:

“Forever. Three hours.”

“My condolences.”

“Ours too.”

Ghost reached the edge of the trench.

“Show me the leg.”

“You a medic?”

“No.”

“Then call the medic.”

Doc raised his good hand.

“Here.”

Philosopher looked over the whole line of them.

“What, you people collect wounded along the road?”

“He does,” Beard said, pointing at Ghost.

“It’s a hobby,” Cashier added.

“Stupid hobby.”

“We told him.”

It took Doc about two minutes to reach the trench. He climbed down carefully and sat beside Philosopher.

“What happened?”

“Fragment. Maybe two. After a hit. Walked at first, then the leg told me it didn’t give a fuck about my plans.”

Doc unwound the outer bandage.

“Did this yourself?”

“Yeah.”

“Not bad.”

“Oh, thank you. Praise from Doc. I can die happy now.”

“Not today.”

Philosopher looked at him.

“Medical opinion?”

“Order. Ghost here commands the impossible. I handle the stupid formalities.”

Ghost sat on the edge of the trench and looked back at the men still standing in the lane.

“Long?”

Doc did not look up.

“Five minutes.”

“Three.”

“Five.”

“Four.”

“Fuck off.”

“Fine.”

Philosopher smiled.

“You two married?”

For the first time all day Doc laughed out loud.

“No. He won’t show me his wound.”

Philosopher looked at Ghost.

“You wounded?”

“Caught something in the side.”

“Serious?”

“No.”

Doc stopped laughing.

“See? Again.”

“You’ll look at the stab point.”

“Heard that before.”

Philosopher looked at both of them.

“Listen, maybe leave me here? At least I’d get some rest from you.”

Ghost said:

“No.”

“Just offering.”

Doc finished the dressing.

“Bone feels intact. Bleeding’s under control. You can put weight on it, but it’s going to be shit.”

“Already is.”

“Then no surprise.”

Philosopher planted his hands and tried to stand.

Didn’t work.

Sat back down.

“That’s it.”

Ghost climbed into the trench.

“Again.”

Philosopher looked at him.

“You always this annoying?”

Pixel called from above:

“Always.”

“I wasn’t asking you.”

“Everybody says that.”

Philosopher grabbed the edge again.

Ghost put a shoulder under him.

“Don’t pull me.”

“I’m not pulling.”

“What are you doing?”

“Standing here.”

“Very useful.”

“Get up.”

Philosopher breathed out and pushed.

This time he made it upright.

He held the right leg almost straight, weight on the left. Stayed there several seconds.

“Well?”

“I’m standing.”

“I can see.”

“What next?”

Ghost pointed ahead.

“Another hundred meters.”

The entire group groaned.

Philosopher looked around at them.

“Did I miss something?”

Beard said:

“You’re still new here.”

Philosopher took a first step.

Then a second.

On the third he grabbed Ghost’s shoulder.

“Fuck.”

“Normal.”

“Don’t say ‘normal.’ I hate that word.”

“Fine.”

“And ‘fine’ too.”

“Then I’ll shut up.”

“Beautiful.”

They climbed out of the trench.

Cashier immediately started counting.

“One, two, three, four, five, six, seven…”

His finger stopped on Philosopher.

“Eight.”

Philosopher looked at him.

“Who are you?”

“Cashier.”

“Oh. Makes sense.”

“What makes sense?”

“Nothing. Just wanted you to feel good.”

Pixel laughed.

“I like him already.”

“You like everybody for the first five minutes,” Beard said.

“Didn’t like you.”

“Mutual.”

Ghost turned back toward the lane.

Now he had to get seven men out.

For some reason that felt easier than bringing six in.

They moved on along the old trench. Philosopher walked between Ghost and Doc. Mongol carried Shorty. Beard helped Pixel across the uneven ground this time. Cashier stayed in the middle and kept counting people with his eyes.

A few minutes later Philosopher asked:

“Why Ghost?”

Ghost did not turn.

“They called me that.”

“Who?”

“The guys.”

“Why?”

“Don’t remember.”

“Not much of a philosopher, are you.”

“And you are?”

Philosopher snorted.

“I’m an electrician.”

Pixel heard him.

“Then why Philosopher?”

“Once told the commander that if we dug a hole long enough we’d come out in China.”

Cashier laughed.

“And?”

“He said China was far, but extra duty was close.”

Beard nodded.

“Wise man.”

“That’s when it stuck.”

“Who’s at home?” Shorty asked.

Philosopher was quiet a moment.

“Mother.”

“Wife?”

“Had one.”

“Divorced?”

“Yeah.”

Shorty nodded.

“Kids?”

“Daughter. Nine.”

“You see her?”

“When they let me.”

The conversation ended by itself.

Philosopher kept walking.

About thirty meters later the lane brought them into a low hollow. No more red signs. Another tree line started ahead.

Ghost stopped everyone.

“Wait.”

Cashier asked:

“What now?”

Ghost listened.

An engine was running somewhere ahead. Unevenly, with pauses. Then a voice. Far enough away that he could not make out words.

Beard said quietly:

“Ours?”

“Don’t know.”

Pixel whispered:

“More intelligence.”

Philosopher leaned toward Ghost.

“There’s a road past the tree line. That’s where I came from. Our people weren’t there.”

“When?”

“Two hours ago. Maybe three.”

Ghost looked at him.

“Why didn’t you go back?”

“Thought forward was closer.”

Pixel said quietly:

“Another member of the Hundred-Meter Sect.”

Ghost crouched.

Far ahead, something moved between the trees. A silhouette. Then another.

Doc whispered:

“Not ours?”

Ghost did not answer.

Nobody needed one.

Mongol could hear nothing, but he saw the faces change. He carefully set Shorty down and took the machine gun off his shoulder.

Shorty said:

“Oh. Finally putting the furniture down.”

Mongol did not hear him.

Ghost motioned downward.

Everybody lay down or crouched.

For several minutes nothing happened.

Then the unfamiliar voices came closer.

Ghost pointed right, along the hollow.

“We go around.”

Beard looked at Philosopher.

“You can walk?”

“Got options?”

“We could leave you here. You asked.”

“Changed my mind.”

They went right.

Slowly. No talking.

Nobody asked anymore how far it was to the stab point.

Mongol carried part of Shorty’s weight. Beard held Pixel. Doc supported Philosopher. Cashier walked last and checked behind at every bend.

Ghost only showed the direction.

After about twenty minutes the unfamiliar voices were gone.

They came out onto a narrow field road. A wrecked sign stood by the shoulder and, about three hundred meters beyond it, concrete blocks were visible.

Pixel squinted.

“What’s that?”

Doc looked.

“Looks like ours.”

Cashier said immediately:

“I’m not loving ‘looks like’ today.”

Ghost watched longer.

There was blue tape on one of the blocks.

“Ours.”

Nobody cheered out loud.

They simply stood a little straighter.

Philosopher sat on the edge of the road.

“That’s it. One minute.”

Ghost came over.

“Get up.”

Philosopher looked up at him.

“Go fuck yourself.”

Ghost smiled.

“Fine.”

“Seriously?”

“One minute.”

Philosopher looked at him suspiciously.

“You’re sick.”

“Maybe.”

Beard sat beside him. Then Pixel. Mongol simply set Shorty on the ground like a bag and sat down himself. Doc lowered himself last.

Cashier stayed standing.

Looked at everyone.

Counted.

“Eight.”

Mongol tilted his head.

“What?”

Cashier held up eight fingers.

Mongol counted.

Then nodded.

“Eight.”

Pixel stretched his legs out.

“Beautiful.”

“What’s beautiful?” Shorty asked.

“Fuck knows. Just felt like saying it.”

Philosopher looked at Ghost.

“And who were you looking for?”

“What?”

“When you were coming this way. You weren’t looking for us. Where were you going by yourself?”

Ghost pointed at the concrete blocks.

“To the stab point.”

“To get your wound looked at?”

“Yeah.”

Doc opened his eyes immediately.

“You hear that? We have witnesses.”

Ghost shook his head.

“Let’s move.”

Philosopher laughed.

“That’s it. Minute’s over.”

He used Doc’s shoulder and got up.

The others rose after him.

Ghost went first.

A few steps behind, Cashier said:

“Eight.”

Beard did not even turn around.

“Oh, fuck off already.”

And they moved toward the blocks.
