EVACUATION, OR ON THE ROAD TO THE STABILIZATION POINT
Viktor Makarchuk · MaVik

Story Six

Mongol

They found Mongol about ten minutes later.

At first Pixel thought he was drunk.

The man was walking toward them along the tree line, clipping branches with his shoulder and drifting left every few steps. Tall, broad-shouldered, machine gun across his chest. His helmet sat crooked. Dried blood streaked his right cheek.

“Oh,” Pixel said. “Another one.”

Beard did not even stop.

“Don’t take him. We’ve got enough already.”

Ghost raised a hand.

“Hey!”

The man kept walking.

“Hey, soldier!”

Nothing.

Pixel looked at Doc.

“Is he deaf?”

Doc said nothing.

Ghost stepped forward and stood directly in his path.

The man noticed him from about five meters away. Jerked so hard he nearly swung the machine gun up.

“Where the fuck did you come from?!”

He was talking very loudly.

Ghost pointed behind himself.

“From there.”

“What?”

“FROM THERE!”

“Don’t shout!”

Behind them Pixel started laughing.

“That’s it. He’s my favorite.”

The man turned toward the laughter but apparently heard none of it.

Doc came closer.

“Look at me.”

“I’m looking.”

“Call sign?”

“Mongol.”

“How long have you been hearing like this?”

Mongol frowned.

“Like what?”

Doc pointed at his ear.

“Badly.”

“Oh. Since the hit.”

“When?”

“Twenty minutes ago. Maybe forty.”

Cashier said quietly:

“Our kind of customer.”

Mongol did not hear him.

Doc stepped almost nose to nose and spoke more slowly.

“Nausea?”

“No.”

“Vomited?”

“No.”

“Dizzy?”

Mongol thought about it.

“Ground moves around a little.”

“Fucking great.”

“What?”

“I said, fucking great.”

“Oh.”

Doc took him by the chin and turned his head. There was an abrasion behind the right ear and dried blood below it.

“Where’s this from?”

“Fuck knows.”

“Ear hurt?”

“No. Just doesn’t work.”

“The other?”

“Like through a pillow.”

Pixel came around to his other side and almost yelled:

“WHAT’S YOUR NAME?”

Mongol turned to him.

“Mongol.”

“I’M PIXEL.”

“Nice to meet you.”

“I’M GLAD YOU CAN HEAR ME.”

Mongol stared at him for a second.

“Why are you shouting?”

Cashier doubled over.

Even Shorty laughed, holding on to Beard.

Pixel looked offended.

“Fuck all of you.”

“What did he say?” Mongol asked.

“Introduced himself,” Ghost answered.

Mongol nodded and started walking past him again.

Ghost grabbed his plate carrier.

“Where are you going?”

“Back.”

“Back where?”

Mongol pointed the way he had just come.

“To my guys.”

“Where are they?”

“There.”

“Where is ‘there’?”

Mongol waved irritably.

“At the position.”

“What position?”

“Our fucking position.”

Ghost did not let go.

Mongol looked at the hand on his armor.

“Take that off.”

“You’re going the wrong way.”

“How do you know?”

“Because the front is that way.”

Ghost pointed behind Mongol’s shoulder.

Mongol froze.

For several seconds he looked that way. Then toward the direction he had been heading.

“No.”

Beard came closer.

“Yes.”

Mongol looked at him.

“Who are you?”

“Beard.”

“What?”

“BEARD!”

“I can see that.”

Pixel burst out laughing again.

Beard wiped his face with a sleeve.

“I’m going to kill him.”

“Get in line,” Doc said.

Mongol stared at the tree line behind him.

“Can’t be.”

Ghost let go of his armor.

“What do you remember?”

“The hit.”

“Before the hit.”

“We were sitting. Then stuff started coming down. I crawled out toward the gun. Another hit. Threw me.”

He touched his helmet.

“Then I got up. Owl was yelling something. I couldn’t hear him. Boar was waving. Then another bang.”

“And after that?”

Mongol was silent.

“After that?”

“Don’t know.”

Doc stepped in front of him so Mongol could see his face.

“Lose consciousness?”

“Don’t know.”

“That means possibly.”

“That means I don’t know.”

“Fine. Where are Owl and Boar now?”

Mongol pointed sharply behind himself.

“There.”

Then his hand hung in the air.

“Probably.”

For the first time he said it more quietly.

Ghost looked at the others.

Pixel had already sat under a tree. Shorty stood leaning on Beard. Cashier held his wounded arm against his chest. Doc was protecting his right hand. Nobody here looked like a man who needed one more person to convince.

Mongol looked them all over.

“Where are you going?”

“To the stab point,” Ghost said.

“Where’s that?”

Pixel raised his head.

“Don’t ask.”

Mongol did not hear.

“What?”

“NOTHING!”

“Don’t shout.”

Pixel lay flat on his back.

“Lord, take me.”

Cashier said:

“Don’t ask. We’ve got Ghost here. Wrong one might hear you.”

This time everybody laughed except Mongol.

He stood staring back.

“I can’t leave.”

Ghost asked:

“Why?”

“My guys are there.”

“Maybe.”

“What do you mean, maybe?”

“Exactly that. You don’t know where they are. You don’t know how long you were down. And you don’t know where you’re going.”

Mongol clenched his jaw.

“I’ll go back.”

“You will.”

Doc looked at Ghost.

Mongol did too.

“Where?”

“First, back to our people. Tell them where you last saw your guys. They’ll sort it out.”

“What if they’re there right now?”

Ghost shrugged.

“Then you help them more by getting there.”

Mongol said nothing.

“What are you going to do here?” Ghost asked.

Silence.

“Walk alone in a direction you already mixed up?”

Mongol slowly exhaled.

“Fuck.”

“Yeah.”

“Hate it when somebody’s smart.”

Beard said:

“Don’t worry. It happens to him by accident.”

Mongol only heard the last word.

“What happens by accident?”

“Everything,” Beard answered.

Doc touched Mongol’s shoulder.

“Give me the machine gun.”

Mongol immediately hugged it closer.

“What the fuck for?”

“Because you’ve been concussed, you’re drifting sideways, and your reaction time is like a refrigerator.”

“I walk fine.”

Everybody silently looked at him.

Mongol looked at all of them.

“Fuck you.”

He did not give up the machine gun.

They moved out.

For the first few minutes Mongol walked last and looked back every twenty steps. Cashier looked back a few times too, but now he was checking on Mongol.

Then Shorty began falling behind.

His leg was holding worse and worse. He did not complain. He simply hung on Beard more often, and Beard himself was limping badly enough that Doc kept looking at him and saying nothing.

Mongol noticed.

Came over.

“What’s wrong with him?”

Shorty answered:

“Everything’s wonderful.”

“What?”

“LEG!”

“I can see.”

Mongol shifted the machine gun into a better position, hooked an arm under Shorty, and hauled him toward himself.

“What are you doing?!”

Mongol did not hear.

“HEY! PUT ME DOWN!”

“Don’t shout.”

“PUT ME DOWN, FUCK!”

Mongol threw Shorty’s arm over his shoulder.

Shorty was almost hanging in midair.

“I CAN WALK!”

“What?”

“MYSELF!”

“Good.”

And he kept walking, still carrying half of him.

Pixel was already covering his mouth with both hands.

“Shorty, don’t argue. He can’t hear you.”

“I can see that!”

“But he also can’t see you as a problem.”

“Pixel, I’ll break your good leg.”

“Get in line.”

For the first time in a long while Beard was walking without somebody else’s weight on his shoulder. He took several steps as if he did not quite believe it.

“Mongol!”

Mongol turned.

Beard gave him a thumbs-up.

Mongol returned it.

“What does he want?” he asked.

“Says he loves you,” Cashier answered.

“He can fuck off.”

“Oh, he hears when it matters,” Doc said.

A few minutes later they stopped.

Not because of Mongol.

Ghost raised a hand.

Ahead, the tree line thinned. Something flashed in the sun beyond the trees. Metal or glass.

Everybody crouched almost at once.

Mongol stayed standing.

Ghost grabbed his shoulder and yanked him down.

“Sit.”

“What for?”

“Just sit.”

Mongol crouched.

“What’s there?”

“Don’t know.”

“Then why are we sitting?”

Cashier said:

“Because when he says ‘don’t know,’ that’s practically intelligence now.”

Ghost slowly raised his head.

Nothing moved ahead.

The flash came from a piece of mirror hanging from the door of a wrecked car farther beyond the trees.

They moved again.

Mongol now walked beside Shorty. Shorty no longer argued. He simply hung on his shoulder and saved strength.

“How’d you get so huge?” Shorty asked.

“What?”

Shorty gestured at his shoulders.

“BIG. HOW?”

“Oh. Moved furniture.”

“Seriously?”

“Seventeen years. Had my own van. Wardrobes, sofas, kitchens. Fifth floor, no elevator—the classic.”

“Now I get why you don’t even feel me.”

“What?”

“Nothing.”

Mongol thought, then said:

“I’ve got two daughters.”

“And?”

“Nothing. You asked why I’m big. Three women at home. Had to survive.”

Shorty laughed.

“How old are your daughters?”

“Twelve and nine.”

“And your wife?”

Mongol looked at him.

“Are you an idiot?”

“Just asking.”

“If I say her age out loud, she’ll somehow find out.”

“Smart man.”

“Alive man.”

Ghost, walking in front, broke stride for one second.

Nobody noticed.

Mongol went on:

“My youngest told me before I left that I had to come back with all my fingers.”

Shorty looked at his hands.

“Plan’s working so far.”

“Yeah.”

“And why Mongol?”

“Long story.”

“We’ve got far to go.”

Pixel called from behind:

“Don’t say ‘far’ around Ghost. He’ll hear it and tell you it’s a hundred meters.”

Mongol turned his head.

“What did he say?”

“That we’re close,” Shorty answered.

“Oh.”

They covered another two hundred meters.

Mongol suddenly stopped.

Everybody stopped too because Shorty nearly went down.

“What?” Ghost asked.

Mongol stared ahead.

“There’s a sign.”

“What sign?”

He pointed.

Between two trees, almost at ground level, hung a dirty red rectangle. Beyond it, already in the open, there was another one.

Doc narrowed his eyes.

“Fuck.”

Cashier came closer, but Ghost immediately held out a hand.

“Stay.”

Mongol did not hear the word, but saw the gesture and froze.

Shorty said quietly:

“Mines?”

Nobody answered.

Pixel looked right, then left.

“We’re not going through there, are we?”

Ghost studied the field.

Something boomed far away.

Mongol did not even turn his head.

“What?” he asked.

Beard sat down on the ground.

“Nothing. Our hundred meters just ran out.”

Cashier automatically began counting people.

His finger moved across Pixel, Beard, Doc, Shorty, Mongol, Ghost, then stopped on himself.

“Seven.”

Mongol leaned toward him.

“What’s seven?”

Cashier looked at the field.

“So far the numbers still balance.”
