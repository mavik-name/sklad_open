EVACUATION, OR ON THE ROAD TO THE STABILIZATION POINT
Viktor Makarchuk · MaVik

Story Two

Beard

It was quieter behind the embankment.

Not quiet—just that the earth swallowed some of the sound. Artillery was working somewhere farther off; every now and then something small and motorized passed overhead. But here, in the narrow hollow between the embankment and the wrecked trench, at least you did not have to shout.

“Anybody alive?!” came the voice from below again.

“Yeah,” Ghost answered. “Where are you?”

“Where do you think?!”

“Fucking great. Another man who likes riddles.”

Pixel limped up behind him and leaned a shoulder against the embankment.

“I told you we shouldn’t go.”

“You said, ‘Maybe we shouldn’t go.’”

“That was a very specific proposal.”

Ghost looked down.

The trench had taken a much worse beating here. One wall had slumped, several roof logs lay at angles, and a black gap showed between them. That was where the voice came from.

“Arms and legs still there?” Ghost shouted.

“Fuck knows. Got both arms. I can see one leg.”

“The other one?”

“If you find it, let me know.”

Pixel gave a quiet snort.

“He’s all right.”

Ghost climbed down.

A man close to fifty was pinned under a log. There was more gray than dark in his beard. His helmet had slipped sideways, his face was caked with dirt, and one arm was trapped between his chest and the trench wall. His right leg was visible down to the knee. Beyond that it disappeared under soil and debris.

When he saw Ghost, the man grimaced.

“Where’s emergency services?”

“Didn’t show.”

“Shit service you’ve got.”

“Leave a review on the way out.”

“One star.”

Ghost crouched beside him.

“Call sign?”

“Beard.”

“Didn’t see that coming.”

“What are you, fucking Sherlock?”

“Ghost.”

Beard blinked.

“Seriously?”

“What?”

“Nothing. Just my luck.”

Pixel called from above:

“Don’t worry. He’s normal so far.”

“I especially liked the ‘so far.’”

Ghost examined what he could see. No blood on the chest. The left sleeve was torn, the forearm underneath scraped raw. Beard’s breathing was steady, though every deeper breath took more effort.

“Head?”

“Still attached.”

“Nauseous?”

“From your company.”

“Then the head’s probably fine too.”

Ghost touched the exposed leg.

“Feel that?”

“Yeah.”

“Move your toes.”

The boot twitched slightly.

“Where’s the other leg?”

“Under this shit. And don’t ask if I can move it. I don’t know.”

Ghost looked up.

The log itself was not the main problem. It was holding a section of the roof that had a load of soil on top of it. Yank the wrong thing and the whole lot could come down.

“How long you been here?”

“Didn’t time it. Since the strike.”

“Which one?”

“What a fucking excellent question.”

Pixel pulled out his phone above them.

“I’ve got 13:24.”

Beard looked at him.

“You up there scrolling TikTok or you gonna help?”

“I’ve got a hole in my leg.”

“Oh. Sorry.”

“No problem.”

“You can still help, though.”

Pixel looked at Ghost.

“See? I love him already.”

Ghost climbed back up and went for the entrenching tool they had left by the bend. Pixel did not follow. When Ghost came back, he was already sitting near the edge of the trench, scraping loose soil away from the log with both hands.

“Did I tell you to sit?”

“No.”

“Then sit.”

“I am sitting.”

“Then why the fuck are you digging with your hands?”

“Because there’s one shovel, genius.”

From below Beard said:

“Don’t fight. I’m in no hurry.”

“You shut up,” both of them answered almost at once.

Beard burst out laughing and immediately groaned.

“Ow, fuck. Don’t make me laugh.”

Ghost began taking the dirt off in thin layers. First from the top, then from the side so the roof section would not shift.

Pixel worked with his hands, tossing clods behind him. After a few minutes he stopped, wiped his forehead, and sat back hard.

“You okay?” Ghost asked.

“Fucking fantastic. I’m on vacation, remember?”

“Then rest.”

“Don’t order me around.”

Beard said quietly:

“Kid, seriously. Sit down.”

Pixel looked down.

“I am sitting.”

“Sit properly. Don’t wreck your other leg because of me.”

“I’ve still got another one.”

“One spare isn’t enough.”

Pixel stopped digging and leaned his back against the wall.

Ghost kept going alone.

A few minutes later the edge of a thick metal sheet appeared across Beard’s leg. Soil had packed underneath it and was crushing the thigh.

“I can see the leg.”

“Still there?”

“Still there.”

“That’s good news.”

“Don’t get ahead of yourself.”

“Fuck.”

Ghost slid his palm under the edge of the sheet.

“Pulling? Cutting? Burning?”

“Right now everything’s burning.”

“Be specific.”

Beard was quiet for a moment.

“Pressure on the thigh. Knee feels intact. Can’t feel the foot properly.”

Ghost pulled his hand back.

“Good.”

“What’s good?”

“Now we know where it is.”

“You are a very positive person.”

“I try.”

Pixel looked at the sheet.

“That thing’s heavy.”

“I can see that.”

“Three of us would be better.”

“There are two and a half of us.”

“Who’s the half?”

Ghost looked at him.

“You.”

“Fuck you.”

“See? Better already. Plenty of energy.”

They cleared the metal around the edge. Beard asked them a few times to leave him and go, though without much conviction.

The third time, Ghost had had enough.

“Beard.”

“What?”

“Tell us to go one more time and I’ll fill your mouth with dirt.”

“I’m just offering an option.”

“Shitty option.”

“There aren’t any others.”

“There is. We pull you out.”

Beard looked up at him.

“How are you so sure?”

Ghost shrugged.

“Because we already started.”

Pixel added:

“He’s generally an idiot. Don’t argue.”

“I figured that out.”

At last the sheet moved.

Ghost lifted one edge. Pixel planted his good leg in the dirt and pulled on a strap they had threaded through a handle.

“One,” Ghost said.

“Don’t start,” Pixel groaned.

“Two.”

“Oh fuck, again…”

“Three.”

The metal shifted about ten centimeters.

Beard screamed.

Ghost dropped the sheet and bent over him at once.

“What?”

“Leg! Fuck…”

“Where?”

“Thigh. Pressure came off. Wait. Don’t touch it.”

He breathed through his mouth, staring into the dirt.

Pixel was silent too.

A minute later Beard moved his foot himself.

“Oh.”

“What?”

“Got it.”

“Got what?”

“The fucking leg. I can feel it.”

“Told you it was there.”

“Fuck you.”

Ghost smiled.

“Then you’re ready to stand.”

“Are you fucking insane?”

Pixel laughed.

“I already told him that.”

To get Beard completely free they had to dismantle another piece of the roof. He helped with his hands as much as he could. When he finally sat upright in the trench, for two full minutes he did nothing but stare at both legs.

Then he tapped his right knee.

“Mine.”

“Checked?”

“Yeah.”

“Show ID.”

“Fuck off.”

Ghost held out a hand.

“Get up.”

Beard looked at him, then at Pixel.

“You two seriously going somewhere?”

“To the stab point,” Pixel said.

“With that leg?”

“Yeah.”

“Far?”

“Don’t ask. He lies about distances.”

Ghost kept his hand out.

Beard sat another second.

“I think I’ve got ribs.”

“Could be.”

“Leg’s fucked too, no idea how bad.”

“We’ll see.”

“Excellent medical exam you run.”

“Free of charge.”

Beard took his hand.

He did not get up on the first try.

When he put weight on the right leg the knee buckled, but held. He stepped carefully with the left. Then once more.

“Not broken,” he said.

“Not necessarily.”

“Don’t jinx it.”

“Then don’t diagnose yourself.”

Beard leaned against the trench wall.

“Got a cigarette?”

Pixel stared at him.

“We just dug you out.”

“And?”

“And your first question is about smoking?”

“What am I supposed to ask?”

Ghost checked his pockets.

“I’ve got none.”

Pixel pulled a crushed pack from his armor.

“One.”

“Give it here.”

“It’s my last one.”

“I’m older.”

“That’s not an argument.”

“I was just buried alive.”

Pixel thought about it.

“That’s an argument.”

Beard lit up. He took the first drag carefully, grimaced, and did not inhale that deeply again. He sat on the edge of the trench, looked at the cigarette, and said nothing.

“How old are you?” Pixel asked.

“Forty-seven.”

“I thought older.”

“Thank you, asshole.”

“It’s the beard.”

“How old are you?”

“Twenty-three.”

“I thought twelve.”

“Fuck you.”

“It’s the glasses.”

Ghost listened while watching the tree line. They needed to move. Staying in one place too long felt wrong.

Beard smoked down to the filter, ground it out in the dirt, and carefully put the butt in his pocket.

Pixel looked surprised.

“What are you doing?”

“Don’t litter.”

“Half the tree line’s blown to shit.”

“So let’s take a dump on it too.”

“Logical.”

Beard stood on his own. Slowly, holding the edge.

“What’d you do before the war?” Pixel asked.

“Was a person.”

“And now?”

“Now I’m Beard.”

“Very informative.”

“Had a garage. Small one. Two lifts. Never did buy the third.”

“Worked on cars?”

“By then I mostly swore at the guys who did.”

“Boss.”

“Slave owner.”

Ghost handed him the rifle he had found under the debris. Beard checked the magazine, racked the bolt, and winced at the pain in his side.

“Your wife running the place alone now?”

“My son.”

“He a mechanic too?”

“No. Designer.”

Pixel turned his head.

“At a garage?”

“Exactly what I keep asking: what the fuck do I need a designer for at a garage?”

“Maybe he’ll make you a logo.”

“Already did.”

“Any good?”

Beard was quiet for a long moment.

“Fuck knows.”

“Means it’s good.”

“He’d charge a stranger twenty-seven thousand for it. Did mine free. Now I can’t tell him it’s shit.”

Pixel laughed.

“Fatherhood.”

“You have kids, you’ll understand.”

Pixel stopped laughing at once.

“We’re not married yet.”

Beard looked at him.

“Who’s ‘we’?”

“Me and Lera.”

“What’s that got to do with me?”

“Nothing.”

“Then get married.”

“He said the same thing.”

Pixel nodded at Ghost.

Beard looked Ghost over.

“You married?”

Ghost adjusted the rifle on its sling.

“Let’s move.”

“Oh. So you are.”

“How’d you get that?”

“You didn’t answer.”

“This an interrogation now?”

“There are two of us and one of you.”

Pixel raised a finger.

“I’m half.”

Beard looked at his leg.

“Fair.”

Ghost started walking first.

“Hundred meters to the next stop?” Pixel called.

“Hundred.”

Beard put a hand on his shoulder.

“Don’t believe him.”

“Already learned.”

They moved out.

For the first twenty meters Beard walked on his own, dragging the left leg slightly. Then he noticed Pixel beginning to sag harder toward his good side.

“Give it here.”

“What?”

“Your arm.”

“Don’t need it.”

“I wasn’t asking.”

“Your ribs are fucked.”

“And your leg is. I’m not carrying you with my ribs.”

Pixel tried to wave him off.

Beard had already put his shoulder under him.

“Come on, kid.”

“Don’t call me kid.”

“Fine, old man. Come on.”

Pixel leaned on him.

Ghost looked back.

Beard walked beside Pixel, holding him under the elbow. He himself grimaced every third step, but did not let go.

“You okay?” Ghost asked.

“Keep walking,” Beard said. “We won’t get lost.”

“You might.”

“Then walk slower.”

Ghost slowed down.

A few minutes later Pixel said:

“Beard.”

“What?”

“The third lift?”

“What about it?”

“You gonna buy it?”

Beard snorted.

“Probably should.”

“That’s it, then.”

“What’s ‘it’?”

“Nothing. Just walk.”

Beard looked at him, but did not ask.

Ahead, the trench ended. Beyond it was forty meters of open ground, then another tree line.

Ghost stopped.

Pixel and Beard caught up.

“What?” Beard asked.

Ghost pointed ahead.

In the grass, almost at the edge of the tree line, a hand lifted for a moment and fell again.

A second later they heard a shout:

“Don’t shoot! Friendly! Friendlies here, fuck!”

Pixel let out a heavy breath.

“Fucking great.”

Beard looked at Ghost.

“Is the whole route like this?”

Ghost flicked the safety off his rifle.

“So far.”

“Then I’m taking back my one star.”

“Too late.”

“Why?”

“Review’s already published.”

Beard laughed quietly and took a firmer hold on Pixel’s arm.

“Come on then, rescuer.”

Ghost stepped out of the trench first.
