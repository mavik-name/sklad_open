EVACUATION, OR ON THE ROAD TO THE STABILIZATION POINT
Viktor Makarchuk · MaVik

Story Four

Shorty

“Two.”

The log did not move.

Doc breathed out through his teeth and stopped pulling.

“Yank like that again and I’ll break your good arm,” Beard said.

“Thank you for the consultation.”

“Free of charge.”

Ghost crouched lower. Under the fallen trunk were dirt, branches, a piece of plastic, and the edge of some kind of roof section. The voice from underneath had gone quiet.

“Hey!” he shouted. “Alive?”

A pause.

“Nah, fuck. Already went home.”

Pixel sat straight down on the ground.

“Oh. One of ours.”

“Where are you pinned?” Doc asked.

“Fuck knows. I can’t see shit from here.”

“Can you feel your legs?”

“One for sure. It’s making a very convincing case that it exists.”

“The other?”

Another pause.

“Yeah. Toes move.”

Doc nodded to himself.

“Good. Don’t move around.”

“What do you think I’m doing down here, dancing?”

Ghost began clearing soil away from the edge of the roof section with his hands. Beard crouched beside him. Pixel crawled closer and pushed a thick branch away with his good leg.

“You sit,” Beard said.

“I am sitting.”

“Then sit properly.”

“Your own leg’s fucked.”

“And you’re missing a brain. Different problem.”

Pixel took offense in silence and dragged away the next branch.

Doc lay on his stomach and pushed his head into the gap.

“Can you see me?”

“I can see some ugly mug.”

“That’s already good.”

“Wouldn’t say so.”

“Name?”

“Shorty.”

Doc turned his head toward the others.

“Shorty.”

“We heard him,” Beard said.

From under the dirt came:

“How many of you are up there?”

Ghost answered:

“Four.”

“Holy fuck. Whole delegation.”

Pixel laughed.

“You’ll be number five.”

“I’m not going anywhere.”

Everybody went quiet for a second.

Doc leaned back toward the gap.

“Why?”

“Leg.”

“You said your toes move.”

“They move. It also hurts so fucking much I’m about to hate every one of you.”

“Not a contraindication.”

“To what?”

“To hate. Go ahead and hate us.”

Ghost found the edge of the roof section. It was a piece of wooden decking pinned from above by the trunk and the earth. Pull it straight up and everything might spill inward.

“We need to move the tree first.”

Beard looked at him.

“With what?”

Ghost looked around.

Nothing.

Beard looked too.

“Beautiful plan.”

“Got a better one?”

“Yeah. Stop letting you make plans.”

Pixel pointed toward the thinner end of the trunk.

“It’ll be easier there.”

“You can see that from here?” Beard asked.

“I’m young. My eyes work.”

“Your leg doesn’t.”

“Can’t have everything.”

They went around the pile from the other side. The trunk was not resting on the roof section with its full weight. A fork of another tree held some of it. They did not need to lift the trunk, only rotate it a little.

Doc looked at his right arm.

“I’m incredibly useful today.”

Ghost took position at the thick end.

“Push with the left.”

“Thanks. Never would’ve thought of that.”

Beard braced his shoulder against it.

Pixel stayed by the gap.

“Shorty!”

“What?”

“It’s about to get noisy.”

“Right, because it’s been so fucking peaceful here.”

“Well, I warned you.”

They pushed.

The trunk did not move at all.

“Again,” Ghost said.

“You know the word ‘rest’?” Beard asked.

“Yeah.”

“And?”

“Later.”

“Asshole.”

The second time the tree creaked.

The third time it rotated a few centimeters and suddenly dropped lower.

The man under the pile screamed.

Ghost jumped back at once.

“Shorty!”

“Here! I’m here, fuck! Don’t touch it!”

Doc was already at the gap.

“What happened?”

“Leg! Nothing. Wait. Wait…”

He was breathing loudly, in broken pulls.

Nobody moved.

Pixel stared at the dirt in front of him.

Beard rested his hands on his knees.

A minute later the voice under the debris said:

“Jesus fucking Christ.”

Doc relaxed a little.

“That’s a good sign.”

“What is?”

“You’re swearing in full sentences.”

“I can do paragraphs if you want.”

“Then we’re alive.”

Ghost went back to the trunk.

“How much farther?”

Doc examined the gap.

“Another ten, fifteen centimeters. Then we can take the boards apart.”

“Okay.”

Shorty heard him.

“No. Guys, don’t.”

Ghost bent down.

“Don’t what?”

“Fuck knows how much more shit is lying on top. You’ll end up under here yourselves.”

“We won’t.”

“How do you know?”

“I don’t.”

“Fucking perfect.”

“Yeah.”

“Get out of here.”

Beard snorted.

“Oh, here we go.”

Shorty went quiet.

“What?”

“I said the same thing.”

“And?”

“Didn’t help.”

Pixel raised a hand.

“I didn’t say it. I figured out right away he was fucked in the head.”

“Who?” Shorty asked.

Pixel pointed at Ghost, then remembered the man could not see him.

“Ghost.”

Silence came from under the earth.

“You’ve got a Ghost up there?”

“Yeah.”

“Fuck. Very reassuring.”

Ghost smiled.

“You coming out or are you reviewing our call signs?”

“If I get out, I’ll leave you a review.”

“Get in line,” Beard said.

The second push moved the tree more easily. Beard immediately sat on the ground and pressed a hand to his side.

Ghost looked over.

“You okay?”

“Fuck off.”

“So you’re okay.”

“You’re learning.”

Doc and Pixel began taking apart what had opened beneath the trunk. Pixel pulled boards while sitting down; Doc worked with his left hand. Ghost threw out dirt.

A few minutes later a helmet appeared.

Then a face.

Shorty was gray all over with dust. Only his teeth were white.

“Handsome bunch,” he said.

“Look who’s talking,” Pixel answered.

“Who are you?”

“Pixel.”

“Did they recruit you out of summer camp?”

“I’m twenty-three.”

“Exactly.”

Beard laughed and immediately grabbed his ribs.

“Don’t make him laugh,” Ghost said.

“Why?”

“He’ll fall apart.”

“I’m remembering every one of you,” Beard said.

Two more boards came out and Shorty’s shoulders became visible. He really was short. Not skinny—just compact, low, almost square-built.

“What’s holding you?” Ghost asked.

“Left leg. Below the knee. Something’s lying on it.”

Doc reached in as far as he could.

“Feel the foot?”

“Yeah.”

“Cold or warm?”

“How the fuck should I know? It’s my foot, I’m feeling it from inside.”

“Fair.”

“You sure you’re a medic?”

“No. I just enjoy asking stupid questions to people under rubble.”

Shorty looked at him.

“Doc?”

“Yeah.”

“Then sorry.”

“Not forgiven.”

The thing on his leg turned out to be a piece of log, much smaller than the one they had already shifted. Ghost and Beard lifted it enough for Doc to pull the leg free.

Shorty screamed obscenities so loudly Pixel actually looked toward the field.

“Keep it down,” he said. “The Russians are gonna hear us.”

Shorty lay with his eyes shut.

“Tell them… I’m busy.”

Then he opened his eyes.

“Leg still there?”

Doc was already looking.

“Still there.”

“That’s good.”

“That’s very good.”

“Can I walk?”

“Don’t know.”

“Fantastic medicine you people have.”

“You haven’t seen the bill yet.”

They pulled him out slowly. Shoulders first, then torso. Halfway through, his plate carrier caught and Shorty began swearing at the carrier, the army, the plate manufacturer, and whoever had invented human shoulders.

Pixel was laughing silently by then.

At last Shorty was on open ground.

For a minute he simply lay there.

Ghost sat beside him.

Doc worked on the leg, checking, feeling, looking. Shorty watched his face.

“Well?”

“What do you mean, ‘well’?”

“Don’t make that doctor face.”

“What face?”

“That one. Where you already know everything and haven’t told the patient yet.”

“I don’t know anything yet.”

“Then yours comes naturally.”

Doc snorted.

“No obvious bleeding. Bone isn’t sticking out. Foot is warm. Toes move. That’s what I can tell you here.”

“And the rest?”

“At the stab point.”

Shorty looked at Ghost.

“Far?”

Pixel cut in immediately:

“Don’t ask him.”

“Why?”

“Everything is a hundred meters to him.”

Beard added:

“We’ve already walked about three kilometers of his hundred meters.”

Ghost did not argue.

Shorty pushed himself onto his elbows.

“I’m not getting up.”

Nobody answered right away.

He said it calmly. Not as a plea. Just information.

“You’ll try,” Doc said.

“I couldn’t feel the leg for twenty minutes. Now I feel it so much I wish I didn’t.”

“That’s information too.”

“Doc, I’m serious.”

“So am I.”

Shorty sat up. Went even paler.

Ghost held out a hand.

“Come on.”

Shorty looked at it.

“You piss everybody off like this?”

Pixel answered for Ghost:

“Yeah.”

“And if I can’t?”

Beard was already getting up from the ground.

“Then you hang on us.”

Shorty looked at his leg.

“You can barely stand yourself.”

“Then don’t gain weight.”

Ghost kept his hand out.

Shorty looked at him.

Doc got up.

“Stand already. I want to see what the leg does under load.”

“And if it does nothing?”

Ghost said:

“Then we think of something else.”

Shorty looked at him again.

And took his hand.

The first attempt failed.

He got about twenty centimeters off the ground and sat back down.

“That’s it.”

“No,” Ghost said.

“I said that’s it.”

“I heard you.”

“Then what?”

“Again.”

“Go fuck yourself.”

“Later.”

Beard took the left side. Doc took the right, with his good arm.

“On three?” Doc asked.

“I hate ‘on three,’” Pixel said.

“Why?”

“With these people, after ‘three’ it always hurts.”

Shorty breathed out.

“Do it.”

The second time he stood.

He barely loaded the left leg. He hung between Beard and Doc, teeth clenched, face wet with sweat.

“I’m standing,” he said.

“We can see,” Doc answered.

“I’m fucking standing.”

This time nobody joked.

Shorty took one step.

Then another.

On the third he nearly fell, but Beard held him.

“Don’t rush.”

“I’m not rushing.”

“You took off like a minibus leaving a stop.”

“I don’t ride minibuses.”

“Rich?”

“Short. People trample me in there.”

Pixel snorted.

Ghost shouldered Doc’s medical pack.

Shorty noticed.

“And you? You in one piece?”

“Mostly.”

“Where’s the ‘mostly’?”

“Caught something in the side.”

Doc’s head turned at once.

“Oh, here we go again.”

“What?”

“He won’t show me.”

Shorty looked Ghost up and down.

“He’s doing the right thing.”

Doc looked surprised.

“Why?”

“Because with that face you’d tell him it needs amputating.”

Beard roared with laughter and grabbed his ribs again.

“Fuck… Shorty, I don’t like you anymore.”

“I didn’t ask you to.”

They moved.

Slower now.

Shorty walked between Beard and Doc. Pixel limped behind them and sometimes put a hand against Shorty’s back when he started tipping over.

Five minutes later Shorty said:

“I’m forty-one.”

“Our condolences,” Beard answered.

“I wasn’t talking to you.”

“Who, then?”

“Nobody. You all keep looking at me like I’m seventeen because of the call sign.”

Pixel protested:

“I’m not looking.”

“You look at everybody like a schoolkid.”

“I’m twenty-three.”

“I’ve seen twenty-three-year-olds. My son’s sixteen and he’s already taller than you.”

Pixel straightened.

“How tall?”

“One eighty-seven.”

“And you?”

“Fuck off.”

“Seriously.”

“One sixty-four.”

Beard nodded.

“Now I get it.”

“What do you get?”

“The call sign.”

“Very difficult riddle, that one.”

Doc asked:

“Your son Shorty too?”

“Nah. That bastard calls me ‘Papa Gnome’ at home.”

Pixel doubled over laughing and nearly sat down.

“Easy, idiot,” Shorty said. “Your leg.”

“Papa Gnome…”

“I promised to beat his ass for it.”

“How’s that going?”

“Sounded more convincing back when he was one fifty.”

Even Ghost laughed.

Shorty heard him.

“What are you laughing at?”

“Nothing.”

“Got kids?”

Ghost walked several steps in silence.

“Yeah.”

Beard said at once:

“Oh. Progress.”

Pixel picked it up:

“Last time he ran away from that question.”

“I did not run away.”

“You said ‘let’s go’ and walked off.”

Shorty nodded.

“Means he’s a normal father. We all do that when the question gets uncomfortable.”

Ghost did not answer.

Ahead, the trees thinned. Through them they could see a churned-up dirt road and another tree line beyond it.

Shorty suddenly stopped.

“Wait.”

Everybody stopped.

“What?” Doc asked.

Shorty dug into a chest pocket and pulled out a phone. The screen was shattered into a spiderweb, but it lit up.

“Oh.”

“What ‘oh’?” Beard asked.

“Alive.”

“The phone?”

“Yeah.”

“That’s a popular technical specification today.”

Shorty pressed a button. Looked at the screen.

“No signal.”

“Nobody’s got any,” Doc said.

Shorty kept staring for another second.

Then smiled.

“What?” Pixel asked.

“Photo.”

“What photo?”

Shorty turned the phone around.

On the lock screen was a civilian boy—ridiculously tall and skinny—with one hand resting on the head of a man who came barely to his shoulder. Both were making stupid faces at the camera.

Across the image someone had written in marker: “THE GNOME AND THE GIANT.”

Pixel cracked first.

Beard second.

Doc stared and shook his head.

Shorty took the phone back.

“Raised that little shit, didn’t I.”

“Handsome,” Beard said.

“Who?”

“You.”

“Fuck off.”

He put the phone away.

And walked on by himself.

Not completely by himself. Beard still held his elbow, Doc stayed beside him. But for the next several steps Shorty was no longer hanging on them.

Ghost noticed and said nothing.

Something boomed dully across the road.

Everybody ducked automatically.

Pixel looked at Ghost.

“Hundred meters?”

Ghost looked at the tree line ahead.

“One fifty.”

Beard actually stopped.

“Holy fuck. He’s recovering.”

Shorty did not understand.

“What’s wrong with him?”

Doc answered:

“Long story.”

“Will we get to the stab point before you finish it?”

Pixel shook his head.

“Nope.”

And they kept walking.
