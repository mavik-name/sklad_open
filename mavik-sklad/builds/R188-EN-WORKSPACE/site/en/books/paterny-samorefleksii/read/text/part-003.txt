SECOND CIRCLE


8. BEING RIGHT

Being right is not always light.
Sometimes it is only a flashlight someone shines into another person's eyes so they cannot see the hands holding it.
I have seen people be so mercilessly right that after hearing their truth, you did not want to repent. You wanted to disappear.
They used exact words.
Down to the comma.
Down to the fact.
Down to the last nail in someone else's coffin.
And still there was no God in their righteousness.
Because God, if He exists, cannot rejoice when truth is turned into a club.
You can tell a person:
"You fell."
Or you can say it in such a way that they will never want to stand up again.
You can call sin a sin.
Or you can press a person's face into their mud for so long that eventually they begin to believe they are the mud.
I am not against truth.
I am against the pleasure with which it is sometimes served at the table.
Like a hot dish made from someone else's shame.
Being right without mercy is a well-sharpened knife in the hands of someone who calls himself a surgeon but enjoys blood too much.
So more and more often I stop myself before the final sentence.
The strongest one.
The one that would finish the job.
And I ask:
what for?
To heal?
Or finally to feel taller than someone else?
Because sometimes the silence after truth matters more than the truth itself.
There may still be room for a person inside it.

9. WOUND

A wound is not always weakness.
Sometimes it is the place through which light first enters a person.
Not ceremonially.
Not beautifully.
Without music.
Simply where the skin could no longer hold, the soul suddenly stops pretending to be armour.
We spend a very long time learning not to hurt.
We keep our face composed. Straighten our backs. Make jokes when what we really want is to sit down in the middle of the room and stare silently at one point.
We say:
"I'm fine."
Sometimes so convincingly that we almost believe it ourselves.
But the body knows.
The night knows.
The empty kitchen knows.
The chair you drop into after a hard day knows more than all your acquaintances combined.
A wound does not ask for a stage.
It asks for honesty.
Not always with other people.
At least with yourself.
To say:
yes, it hurts.
Yes, I am not made of iron.
Yes, I too wanted someone, just once, to come not for advice, not for usefulness, not for an answer, but simply for me.
Because the most frightening thing is not that a person is wounded.
It is when they begin to take pride in feeling nothing anymore.
Then the wound becomes a wall.
And a wall, as we know, protects very well.
And hugs very badly.

10. FORGIVENESS

Forgiveness is not a white tablecloth thrown over a dirty table.
Not an amnesty for the knife.
Not a gentle suggestion that the victim should understand the executioner.
I do not like it when forgiveness is used to silence people who have not even been heard yet.
"Forgive," they tell the person whose hands are still shaking.
"Let it go," they tell the one still standing in ashes, unable to tell where their house once was.
Forgiveness cannot be demanded.
It cannot be accelerated.
It cannot be scheduled somewhere between dinner and Sunday prayer.
Forgiveness is when pain loses the right to control your hand.
It does not disappear.
It does not become beautiful.
It does not turn into a wise parable for somebody else's inspiration.
One day you simply understand:
I no longer want to carry this person inside me as an internal dictator.
I do not excuse them.
I do not forget.
I do not invite them back.
But I no longer feed them with my blood.
Maybe that is forgiveness.
Not a gift to the guilty.
But returning the keys to yourself.

11. FREEDOM

Freedom is not being allowed to do anything you want.
That is a child's idea of freedom, belonging to someone who has never been left alone with consequences.
Freedom begins where you can, but do not have to.
You can strike—and you do not.
You can lie—and you remain silent until you find a truth inside yourself that has no poison in it.
You can leave—and you stay because someone is holding on through your presence.
You can stay—and you leave because love became a cage with a soft pillow a long time ago.
Freedom is not escape from every shore.
Water without shores is not always a sea.
Sometimes it is simply a flood.
Freedom needs boundaries.
Not fences built by someone else.
Your own shores.
The ones you do not cross not from fear, but out of respect for the person you still want to remain.
A person without boundaries thinks they have become God.
In reality, they often become the weather conditions for someone else's destruction.
Real freedom is quiet.
It does not shout:
"I have the right!"
It asks:
"What will happen to the other person when I use that right?"

12. HOME

Home is not walls.
A cell has walls too.
Home is the place where your silence is not treated as an insult.
Where you can walk into a room without armour.
Set your exhaustion on the table.
Take the day off your shoulders like a wet coat and not explain why it is so heavy.
Home is when someone knows what you are really like and still does not leave your weakness standing in a draught.
A home does not have to be warm all the time.
People argue.
Doors slam.
Words sometimes hit the floor and break worse than dishes.
But a home exists as long as, after all that, someone still leaves the hallway light on.
Not for the winner.
Not for the one who was right.
Not for the one who explained their behaviour beautifully.
Simply for one of their own.
Because being one of your own does not mean being without sin.
It means your return still has an address.

13. SHAME

Shame is a very old guard.
It stands beside the door and often mistakes sin for the living person who committed it.
Sometimes it is necessary.
When you truly crossed a line.
When you took what belonged to someone else.
When you betrayed trust.
When you said a word after which grass will not grow in somebody's soul for a long time.
But there is another kind of shame.
Not God's.
Human.
Sticky.
Shame for not being the person someone found convenient.
For hurting.
For crying.
For wanting love not as a reward, but as bread.
For not enduring.
For being tired of staying strong in a world that loves using strong people.
That shame needs to be driven out.
Do not pray to it.
Do not listen to it as though it were a prophet.
Do not let it call your soul indecent.
Because there are things a person truly should be ashamed of.
And there are things for which the shame belongs to those who taught that person to be ashamed of being alive.

14. LIGHT

Light is not decoration.
Not an effect in the final scene.
Not a golden frame around the correct face.
Light is work.
Daily.
Unnoticed.
With no guarantee that anyone will say, "Thank you. Today you kept the darkness from becoming a little larger."
Light does not always look beautiful.
Sometimes it has tired eyes.
A bad mood.
Hands that no longer want to hold anyone, and keep holding anyway.
Sometimes light is a person standing knee-deep in darkness themselves, refusing to let someone else drown completely.
I do not believe in bearers of light who have no shadow.
Either they are lying, or they have not yet lived long enough to meet their own night.
Real light does not deny darkness.
It knows its smell.
Its weight.
Its ability to sit down beside you and speak in a voice almost identical to your own.
That is why the person carrying light is not always smiling.
And does not always speak gently.
Sometimes they simply clench their teeth, stand up, find one more crumb of warmth inside themselves, and carry it to a place where nobody has expected anything for a long time.
Perhaps the world is not held together by great righteous people.
But by those who could have become darkness today—and did not.
