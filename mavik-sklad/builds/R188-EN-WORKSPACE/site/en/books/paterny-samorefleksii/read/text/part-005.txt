FOURTH CIRCLE


22. EXHAUSTION

Exhaustion is not always wanting to sleep.
Sometimes you no longer even want that.
You simply want the world to stop demanding something from you for half an hour.
Not ask.
Not tug at your sleeve.
Not place another problem on the table with the expression of someone convinced you were born specifically to solve it.
Exhaustion has many faces.
There is the exhaustion of the body.
It is honest.
It settles into the shoulders, the eyes, the knees, the back.
You can put it to bed.
Cover it with a blanket.
Give it tea.
And then there is exhaustion of the soul.
You cannot put that down.
It follows you through the flat.
Sits beside you at the table.
Opens messages with you.
Looks at the people to whom you still owe some explanation.
And says quietly:
"God, again?"
We are often ashamed of exhaustion.
As if it means we held the line badly.
As if a strong person must be an endless battery in somebody else's flashlight.
But exhaustion is not always defeat.
Sometimes it is the last honest signal from the one you have used for years like a workhorse.
Yourself.
You are allowed not to be able.
Not to answer immediately.
Not to be wise on the first phone call.
Not to rescue everyone who was already drowning before you were born.
Because sometimes the most moral thing a person can do is sit down.
Quietly.
Without explanation.
And finally stop betraying their own exhaustion.

23. CHOICE

Choice is not always two roads.
Sometimes there is no road at all.
There is a swamp.
There is night.
There are boots that have been letting in water for a long time.
And there is you, standing in the middle of all this, trying to understand whether any direction here is still human.
We love beautiful stories about choice.
About crossroads.
About signs.
About an inner voice that solemnly says:
"Go that way."
In real life, the inner voice often wheezes, swears, coughs, and is not entirely certain it has not led you into yet another piece of stupidity.
But a choice still has to be made.
Because refusing to choose is also a choice.
Only a more cowardly one.
The kind whose hands stay clean only because they have spent the whole time in their pockets.
A person does not become themselves in thought.
In thought, all of us are better.
Nobler.
Smarter.
With the correct answers to everyone else's mistakes.
A person becomes themselves in the moment when they could act basely and do not.
When silence could save them, but they speak.
When they could hide behind the crowd, but step forward and say:
"It was me."
Choice rarely looks like victory.
More often it looks like a small loss.
Of comfort.
Of advantage.
Of somebody else's approval.
Of the illusion that you are still good for everyone.
But perhaps a person you are no longer ashamed to meet in the mirror begins precisely with losses like these.

24. SOMEONE ELSE'S PAIN

Someone else's pain is inconvenient.
It arrives without an invitation.
Sits down opposite you.
Ruins the conversation.
Does not let you finish your coffee in peace and explain to yourself that everyone has their own life.
Someone else's pain is not always beautiful.
It is not always grateful.
Not always polite.
It does not always use the right words.
Sometimes it is rude.
It bites.
It pushes away the hand it had just been searching for in the dark.
Because pain rarely has good manners.
It never took a communication course.
Never read books on healthy dialogue.
It simply hurts.
And that is already hard enough.
We often want another person's pain to be convenient.
To explain itself.
To say thank you.
Not interfere.
Not ask too much.
Not force us to change our plans, our tone, our view of ourselves.
But real mercy begins where you see a person not in their best version.
Not dressed for a celebration.
Not edited.
Not the version that has already understood everything and now speaks wisely about what happened.
The version lying among the wreckage and capable only of breathing.
And you do not ask:
"What did you do wrong?"
You do not explain:
"I told you so."
You do not shove a grand philosophy into a small fresh wound.
You simply sit down beside them.
And for a while become a human wall between them and the final darkness.
Perhaps this is how God sometimes works through the hands of ordinary people who have not even had time to wash their morning coffee cup.

25. JUSTICE

Justice is not always revenge that learned respectable vocabulary.
Though that is often how it is presented.
With the right intonation.
With references to principles.
With the face of a person who has been waiting a very long time for permission finally to strike and call it restoring order.
I understand that desire.
When you have been trampled, it is hard not to dream that one day someone will put the offender in exactly the same place.
The same floor.
The same cold.
The same shame caught in the throat.
You want them finally to understand.
But the trouble is that pain is a bad teacher.
It often explains nothing.
It merely produces students who later open schools of cruelty of their own.
Justice is necessary.
Without it, the world becomes a marketplace where the strong always get a discount on another person's dignity.
But justice must be sober.
Not drunk on our resentment.
Not hungry for another person's humiliation.
Not the kind that, after the sentence, secretly regrets that it was not harsher.
Justice is when evil is called evil.
When responsibility does not slip out through the back door.
When the victim is finally told:
"You did not imagine it.
It really was wrong."
But justice does not have to become an executioner in order to prove the executioner was wrong.
Because then it does not return balance to the world.
Only a new knife with a more respectable biography.

26. BOUNDARY

A boundary is not always a wall.
Sometimes it is a thin line drawn inside you that only you can see.
And that is exactly why it is so easy to betray.
No one will notice.
No one will say:
"You just crossed yourself."
No one will stop you.
Quite the opposite—they may praise you.
They will say:
"Good. You finally became practical."
"Don't be naive."
"Everyone does it."
"That's life."
A strange excuse—"that's life."
As if life personally asked us to become small.
A boundary is not always about other people.
Sometimes it is about who you refuse to become even when you have every right.
Not answering baseness with baseness.
Not turning your wound into a national flag.
Not turning someone else's guilt into a lifetime subscription to your own righteousness.
A boundary is when you say:
"No."
Sometimes to another person.
Sometimes to yourself.
To yourself is harder.
Because no one can deceive you as intelligently as you can.
With arguments.
With psychology.
With a history of trauma.
With a beautiful phrase:
"I'm only protecting myself."
When in truth you stopped protecting yourself a long time ago.
You are taking revenge.
Or using someone.
Or running away.
Or keeping a person beside you not with love, but with the fear of no longer being needed.
A boundary is not coldness.
It is a way not to turn intimacy into occupation.
And not to let other people's feet walk across the place inside you where something living is still growing.

27. FAITH

Faith is not always certainty.
Sometimes faith is when certainty is already gone, and you still refuse to become a beast completely.
Not because you have proof.
Not because you understand the divine plan well.
A person who says they understand the divine plan well is either a saint or dangerous.
More often the second.
Faith does not always stand inside a church.
Sometimes it sits in the kitchen at three in the morning.
In an old T-shirt.
With cold tea.
With the eyes of someone who no longer asks for a miracle because they do not have the strength to explain to themselves afterwards why it failed to come again.
And still, somewhere inside, they do not switch off the last little bulb.
Small.
Ridiculous.
Almost absurd.
But theirs.
Faith is not an answer to every question.
Sometimes it is the honesty not to pretend there are no questions.
Not to fill the cracks with the gold of quotations.
Not to shout "glory" when inside you have nearly fallen apart into syllables.
Faith that has been forbidden to cry quickly becomes scenery.
And scenery looks beautiful from a distance.
But it does not keep you warm when night sits down beside you and lays its head on your shoulder.
Perhaps faith begins not where a person finally stopped doubting.
But where they brought their doubt to God and did not run from the shame of it.
Said:
"I don't understand.
I'm angry.
I'm tired.
But if You are there—do not let me become what I myself despise."

28. MEANING

Meaning is not always a great answer.
Sometimes it is a small reason to get up.
Feed the cat.
Write a sentence.
Make coffee for someone who is still asleep.
Go outside and notice that a tree, without any philosophy at all, is making leaves again.
We often search for meaning as if it should arrive in an expensive suit.
Knock on the door.
Introduce itself.
Place a folder with official stamps on the table and say:
"Here is your purpose.
Sign here.
Live accordingly."
But meaning, the bastard, often arrives in slippers.
Through little things.
Through someone who said:
"I'm glad you're here."
Through a text you had not planned to write, but which suddenly began writing you.
Through pain that did not become beautiful, but stopped being useless.
Meaning does not always explain suffering.
Thank God for that.
Because some explanations would insult the people who suffered.
Meaning must not turn a wound into a souvenir.
It can simply give a person, one day, the ability to carry not only themselves out of the darkness.
But someone else too.
At least one person.
Even if only by the sleeve.
Even if only in silence.
Perhaps that is enough.
Not for a grand theory.
Not for a monument.
Not for a textbook on how to live correctly.
But enough to look at yourself in the evening without hatred and say:
"Today I did not understand everything.
I did not endure everything.
I did not do everything well.
But I still have not betrayed the light."
