PATTERNS OF SELF-REFLECTION

AUTHOR'S INTRODUCTION

I don't know whether a person can be taught
to see themselves honestly.

Maybe not.

Because honesty with yourself is not a skill,
not a pose,
not a beautiful sentence for an introduction.

It is inner work without an audience.
Without applause.
Without any guarantee
that you will feel better afterwards.

This collection is not about holiness.

Not about being right.

Not about how one ought to live,
love,
believe,
forgive,
remain silent,
or carry light.

I don't want to stand above another person
holding a lantern
and explain where they went astray.

I have walked the dark corridors
of my own soul often enough,
and I did not always have anything better
than a match in my hand.

These texts are not sermons.

They are an attempt
to look at a human being without make-up.

At their goodness—without sugar.

At their evil—without theatrical horns.

At love—without ownership.

At faith—without decorative certainty.

At guilt—without self-destruction.

At pain—without the desire
to turn it into a medal.

At light—without the illusion
that the one who carries it
is always full of light themselves.

I called this collection
Patterns of Self-Reflection
because each of us carries recurring patterns inside.

Again and again,
we return to the same fears,
resentments,
hopes,
mistakes,
excuses,
prayers,
and silences.

Circumstances change.

People change.

Years change.

And inside us,
the same invisible mechanisms
often keep working.

Self-reflection is not obsessive self-examination.

Nor is it a luxury
for people with nothing better to do.

It is a way
not to become your own wound.

Not to become a knife
just because someone once cut you.

Not to call your exhaustion wisdom,
your harshness truth,
your resentment dignity,
or your power love.

These free-verse texts do not provide answers.

This collection probably should not be read
all at once.

Not because it is difficult.

Because some texts
need silence after them.

They are better taken in doses.
One at a time.
A few at a time.

Like a conversation
after which you do not immediately want
to start another one.

Because self-reflection is not a marathon
and not an endurance contest.

It is a slow entering
into rooms no one has cleaned for a long time,
but where something important
is still being kept.

If one of these texts catches you,
do not hurry on.

Perhaps real reading
will begin precisely
in that pause.

DON'T CALL IT MORALITY

I don't believe in evil
as an independent force.

Evil is not a kingdom.
Not a black throne.
Not horns above a door.

Evil is a place
someone carried goodness out of
and forgot to bring it back.

Like cold,
which has no heart of its own
and simply comes
where warmth has grown tired.

Like darkness,
which does not defeat light,
but merely waits
until someone stops carrying it.

People who speak loudly about morality
do not interest me much.

Usually they hold it in front of themselves
like a placard at a demonstration,
so no one can see their hands.

And the hands are what matter.

What did you touch another person's pain with?
What did you take?
What did you fail to return?
Whom did you hold
when you should have let go?
Whom did you let go
when you only needed to stay nearby?

Love is not always fire.

Sometimes love
is a chair beside a hospital bed.

A glass of water
without a grand speech.

A message:
I'm here.

Written when nothing can be fixed anymore,
but one thing is still possible:
not leaving someone alone.

I don't trust saints
who have never fallen.

They are too clean
to know the price of the ground.

I feel closer to the one
who came out of the thorn bushes
scratched,
angry,
carrying an experience
no parable can make beautiful.

Because sometimes a mistake
becomes the only map.

Sometimes a wound
explains the road
better than a parent's prohibition.

Sometimes a person has to cross the line
to understand
why the line was there.

I don't want to judge the world
from a high bank.

I am standing in the water myself,
knee-deep.

And I don't always know either
where the mud ends
and the bottom begins.

You simply begin to understand with age:

not everything that shines
is light;

not everything that is silent
is empty;

not everyone who made a mistake
is a traitor;

not everyone who is right
is human.

You can win an argument
and lose your soul.

You can tell the truth
in such a way
that afterwards
nothing living remains.

You can love so fiercely
that you suffocate love
with your own embrace.

So I believe less and less
in loud declarations.

And more and more
in quiet presence.

In bread on the table.

In a door
not closed against the weak.

In a word
that did not deliver the final blow.

In the pause
where someone could have said too much,
but chose silence.

Because morality is not
standing above someone
with the correct words.

Morality is having enough strength
not to use another person's weakness
for your own advantage.

Even when no one is watching.

Especially then.
