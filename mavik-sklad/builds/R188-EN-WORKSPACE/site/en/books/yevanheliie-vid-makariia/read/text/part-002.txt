CHAPTER FIVE — OF FORGIVENESS AND STRENGTH

1 And the Creator said to Macarius as he sat among the ashes of human words:
“Do you know what is stronger than a sword? Forgiveness. For the sword divides, but forgiveness heals.”
2 And Macarius said: “But how can one forgive the one who betrayed, who struck, who forgot?”
3 And the voice answered:
“For the one who betrayed is already in darkness. But you are light. And light does not fight darkness; it changes it.”
4 And there came to Macarius a man who had killed his brother, and his eyes were like stone, and his heart like a night without stars.
5 And he said: “I do not ask forgiveness, for I do not believe in it.”
6 And Macarius answered: “You need not believe. Forgiveness is not for you, but for the one who gives it — so that he does not become you.”
7 And the man fell upon his face, for he could not endure the silence, which was stronger than a cry.
8 And Macarius laid his hand upon his head — not as a judge, but as a brother.
9 And he said: “I have not forgotten what you did. But I will not allow it to live within me. For I am not your shadow; I am my own silence.”
10 And the Creator said:
“Forgiveness does not erase; it sets free. It does not forget; it carries. And the one who has forgiven is not weak, but one who has conquered himself.”
11 And Macarius wrote:
“Let forgiveness be like water: it has no shape, yet it has power. And let everyone who carries pain remember: he is not obliged to love the one who hurt him, but he is obliged not to become him.”

CHAPTER SIX — OF THE APOSTLE’S FORGIVENESS

1 And it came to pass that one of the apostle’s disciples took his word and carried it into other houses — not as a blessing, but as plunder.
2 And that disciple became renowned among men, but not before the Creator.
3 And the brothers came to the apostle and said: “Are you not angry that your word has become another man’s?”
4 And the apostle said: “The word is not mine, but His Who gave it to me. And if it lives, let it live, even in unrighteous hands.”
5 And in the night, while all slept, the apostle prayed.
6 And he said within his heart: “Creator, I cannot forgive, for my pain is great.”
7 And he heard a voice:
“You need not forgive as a man. Forgive as one who no longer carries the wound as grievance.”
8 And the apostle wept. And his tears were like dew upon stone.
9 And he rose, and wrote to his disciple:
10 “You took the word, but you did not take the spirit. You speak, but you do not hear. I keep no anger, for anger is shadow, and I am light. And if you return, you will find no condemnation — but neither will you find glory.”
11 And he sealed the scroll with wax and hid it far away.
12 For he said: “He who seeks forgiveness shall find it. He who does not seek it — let him go his own way.”
13 And there was peace in the apostle’s heart, for he had not conquered his brother, but himself.

CHAPTER SEVEN — HOW THE DISCIPLE FELL INTO SORROW AND SOUGHT THE WAY BACK

1 And it came to pass that the disciple who had taken the apostle’s word felt emptiness within his heart.
2 For he spoke to the people, and they listened, but they did not change.
3 And his fame was like grass: it rose quickly, but had no root.
4 And in the night, while all slept, he sat alone and said: “I have a voice, but no truth. I have a word, but no peace.”
5 And he remembered the apostle, and fear took hold of him, for he understood that he had betrayed not a man, but a way.
6 And he rose and went into the wilderness, seeking the tracks of his teacher.
7 And for three days he walked, neither eating nor speaking, and his heart was like a shattered jar.
8 And on the fourth day he saw a cell, and the light within it was like dawn.
9 And he drew near, but did not enter. And he stood and said: “I am not worthy to be here. But if he comes out, I shall fall upon my face.”
10 And the apostle came out and saw his disciple, and said nothing.
11 And the disciple fell upon his face, and his tears were like water upon stone.
12 And the apostle laid his hand upon him and said:
“You did not return the word. But you returned yourself. And that is greater.”
13 And he led him into the cell, and neither questioned nor reproached him.
14 For the one who seeks the way is already upon it.
15 And there was peace between them — not as between teacher and disciple, but as between brothers who had passed through shadow.

CHAPTER EIGHT — OF THE CITIES THAT FORGOT SHAME

1 And there were two cities — Sodom and Gomorrah — standing between river and sand, between flesh and shadow.
2 And there lived people who had forgotten that there is a boundary, and that there is shame, and that there is remembrance of Him Who made them.
3 And their hearts became like stone that takes in no water, and their eyes like windows that see no light.
4 And there was in them neither mercy, nor fear, nor shame. And each sought not his brother, but flesh.
5 And every night was like a market where the soul was sold for a moment of desire.
6 And the voice of the Creator was not heard there, for they drowned it beneath cries of revelry and laughter without roots.
7 And the Creator sent fire — not as wrath, but as cleansing. For the earth cannot bear that which carries death.
8 And those cities fell like a tree dried at the root, and there was none to weep for them, for their tears were false.
9 And only smoke remained, rising toward heaven as witness that there is a boundary, and there is truth, and there is He.
10 And the Creator said:
“Let those who live remember that not every freedom is a path, and not every desire is light.”
11 And Macarius wrote this, that it might not be forgotten between generations: shame is not a burden, but a guard.
12 And he said:
“He who has no shame has no root. And he who has no root bears no fruit.”
13 And there was silence. And in that silence — truth.

CHAPTER NINE — OF THE WATER THAT REMEMBERS

1 And it came to pass in those days when the human heart grew deaf, and memory became like stone that lets no light within.
2 And every man went his own way, asking neither whether it was a path nor whether it was an abyss.
3 And the Creator looked upon the earth and saw in it no reflection of Himself.
4 And He said:
“I gave you breath, and you turned it into a cry. I gave you love, and you turned it into trade. I gave you the earth, and you turned it into a field of blood.”
5 And the Creator sighed, and His sigh became water.
6 And the water rose — not as wrath, but as memory. For water is the eyes of the Creator weeping for creation.
7 And He chose one — a worthy man, who walked not among men, but among silence.
8 And He said to him:
“Build an ark — not as an escape, but as a witness. For I do not destroy; I cleanse.”
9 And the man listened and built, and every plank was like a prayer, and every nail like a tear.
10 And they laughed at him and cast stones, but he was silent, for he knew: when water speaks, stone is silent.
11 And the water came — not from heaven, but from the deep. For heaven is a mirror, and the deep is the heart.
12 And the heart of the earth opened, and water came forth like a memory that could remain silent no longer.
13 And the ark sailed — not as a ship, but as a word seeking one who would hear it.
14 And within it were not only animals, but fear, and hope, and a silence without a name.
15 And for forty days there was rain, and for forty nights — silence.
16 And every drop was remembrance, and every rescue a choice.
17 And when the waters withdrew, and the earth showed its face again, what had been before was no more.
18 For memory is not what remains unchanged, but what changes us.
19 And the Creator said:
“I shall not forget, but I shall not repeat. For I am not wrath; I am the way. And let the rainbow be a sign — not for Me, but for you. That you may remember: water can carry death, but also life.”
20 And Macarius wrote:
“The Flood is not an ending, but a beginning. For when the world drowns, truth floats. And the one who holds to it shall not perish.”
