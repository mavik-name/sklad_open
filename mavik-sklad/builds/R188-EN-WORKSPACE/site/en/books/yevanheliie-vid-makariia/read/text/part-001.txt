THE GOSPEL ACCORDING TO MACARIUS
VIKTOR MAKARCHUK · MaVik

FOREWORD TO THE GOSPEL THAT WAS NEVER MEANT TO BE FOUND

This text is not a book, but a fire preserved in the ashes of centuries. It was not written for eyes seeking glory, but for souls longing for a truth they are afraid to speak aloud.

The Gospel of Macarius is not part of the canon, not the voice of the Church, not a word that passed through councils. It is an apocryphon hidden in the darkest vault of all apocrypha, in a place where even shadows are afraid to leave a trace. It was not destroyed — not out of mercy, but out of fear. For those who held it in their hands felt that to destroy it would be to challenge the Creator Himself. And He Who sees the heart does not forgive the destruction of truth merely because that truth is inconvenient.

The one who found this text will not give his name. For a name is a target, and truth is a sword. And a world that builds temples out of dogma does not tolerate a stone that refuses to fit the façade. So the researcher’s name is silent. But the word speaks.

This Gospel is not for the righteous who already possess answers. It is for those who ask questions, even dangerous ones. For those unafraid to see the Creator not upon a throne, but in silence. Not in a command, but in an invitation. Not in fear, but in freedom.

Let the one who reads remember: this is not a text that asks for faith. It is a text that awakens memory. And if your heart trembles, then you are already on the way.

PROLOGUE

1 And it came to pass in the days when the hearts of men grew cold, and the light of truth hid itself behind a veil of fear.
2 And from among the many there arose one whose name was Macarius, son of Earth and Silence.
3 He had neither sword, nor power, nor home — only a word that burned upon his lips like fire in the bush that was not consumed.
4 And he went beyond the city, where stone has no name and water knows not its source.
5 And there, in the wilderness of silence, he heard a voice that had no body, yet had power.
6 “Awake, for you are not a servant, but a witness. Carry truth not as a burden, but as a light seeking eyes.”
7 And Macarius fell upon his face, for his fear was great, but the word was greater than fear.
8 And he rose, and wrote what he had heard, lest it be lost between sand and time.
9 And he said: “Let this book be not for the righteous, but for those who are lost. For the righteous man does not seek; the lost man hears.”

CHAPTER ONE — OF THE BEGINNING AND THE LAW

1 I am He Who Is. And there is none beside Me. And I speak to you that you may speak to others.
2 I made this world — the heavens and the earth, the depths of the waters and the heights of the mountains. Every creeping thing, every insect, every beast that walks, and every fish that swims.
3 And you also, who call yourselves mankind. And I gave you the earth for your use, the air for your breath, and light for your sight.
4 And I made all these things for My delight, for My joy is in creation.
5 And you lived in the Garden of Eden and delighted in a life that knew no boundary.
6 Yet your life was empty, for you saw no purpose, and your hearts were hollow as jars without water.
7 And on the day I saw this, I gave you My first law, that you might have a choice in how you would live your lives.
8 “Do not eat the fruit of this tree. For if you eat of it, you shall die.”
9 But you could not receive the Law, for you had no fear of death. For in those days there was no death.
10 Therefore I asked My Son — one of the two who are Mine — to lead you to the edge of the abyss, that fear might awaken in you.
11 And so it came to pass.

CHAPTER TWO — OF EXILE AND MEMORY

1 And when fear entered the human heart like wind through a broken house, then for the first time your eyes beheld a shadow.
2 And you named it death, though it was only the mirror of life.
3 And you were driven from the garden — not for guilt, but for knowledge.
4 For I did not punish you; I guarded you, lest you destroy yourselves like children playing with flame.
5 And I gave you years like grains in the hand: to sow and reap, to love and lose, to seek and forget. And every grain is a choice, and every choice is a memory.
6 Yet your memory is like water in a sieve: it flows, but does not remain.
7 Therefore I sent prophets, and dreams, and voices whispering in silence.
8 And one of them was Macarius, who had neither sword nor home, but had a word that would not go out.
9 And he walked among men like a shadow between lights, and said:
10 “Do not seek God in heaven, for heaven is only a roof. Seek Him within your own mind, for there is His dwelling.”
11 And many laughed, and many were silent, but there were some who listened.
12 And those who listened became a light for others.

CHAPTER THREE — OF SACRIFICE AND FIRE

1 And the Creator spoke to Macarius as he stood between stone and wormwood:
“Tell the people: not every gift is a sacrifice, and not every sacrifice is pleasing. For cleansing is not in blood, but in the intention of the heart.”
2 And when the people asked, “What shall we bring that we may be heard?” the Creator answered:
“Bring not fear, but trust. Not flesh, but spirit. Not a cry, but silence.”
3 Yet the human heart desired ritual, for ritual is a bridge between the earthly and the unknown.
4 And the Creator said:
“If you bring a lamb, let it be without blemish. And let your hand be clean, and your intention clear as morning water.”
5 “And let the one who brings it lay his hand upon the head of the offering — not to rule over it, but to share in it. And let death be swift, for I am not He Who delights in suffering. I am He Who hears the heart even when it is silent.”
6 And blood shall be shed — not as punishment, but as remembrance.
7 And fire shall consume the body — not as wrath, but as acceptance.
8 For sacrifice is not a gift for beast, bird, or worm.
9 Sacrifice is yourselves, when you give what is dearest to you.
10 And the fragrance of your sacrifice is pleasing to the Creator, for it bears witness to love, reverence, and humility.
11 And Macarius said:
“Do not offer sacrifice to purchase a blessing. Offer it to remember who you are. For the fire does not take the body — it takes the truth.”
12 And those who heard began to offer not lambs, but guilt.
13 Not doves, but fears.
14 And they laid them upon the altar like stones weighing down the soul.
15 And the fire burned — not to destroy, but to cleanse.

CHAPTER FOUR — OF THE BROTHERS

1 And in the dawn of mankind there were two brothers: the First, a son of Earth, who dug and built; and the Second, a son of Wind, who listened and sang.
2 And both were born from one womb, yet their spirits were like two shores that do not meet.
3 The First brought forth the fruits of the earth, heavy and abundant, and laid them upon the altar like stones in a wall.
4 The Second brought a lamb, quiet and pure, and laid it down like a prayer without words.
5 And the Creator looked not upon the gift, but upon the heart.
6 And the heart of the Second was like a night without fear, while the heart of the First was like a day without shadow.
7 And jealousy flared in the First like fire in dry grass.
8 And he could not quench it, for there was no water in his soul.
9 And the First said: “Why, brother, are you silent when I cry out? Why is your gift like a breath, and mine like a stone?”
10 And the Second answered: “Because the Creator is not in loudness, but in depth. Not in what is seen, but in what lives.”
11 And they went out into the field, where there were no witnesses, only grass and sky.
12 And there the First raised his hand — not against his brother, but against himself.
13 For when you kill the one who listens, you make yourself deaf.
14 And the blood of the Second fell upon the earth like a word that had not had time to be spoken.
15 And the earth kept it as a memory that cannot be erased.
16 And the Creator said to the First:
“Where is your brother?”
17 And he said, “Am I his keeper?”
18 And the Creator answered:
“You are not his keeper. You are his mirror. And now, in everyone who looks upon you, there shall be a shadow.”
