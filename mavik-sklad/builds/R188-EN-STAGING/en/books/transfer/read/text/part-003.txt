I liked the work.

Most of the clients were polite, educated and interesting. A private transfer has a strange property: after a few hours on the road, the person in the back seat stops being just a “client”. Especially when they accidentally discover that the driver writes books.

“You’re seriously a writer?”

“In my time off from the steering wheel.”

After that, the barrier between “client” and “service staff” usually disappeared somewhere, and there were more topics for conversation than kilometres left to the destination.

In the entire month, I do not think I met a single truly unbearable passenger.

Not even celebrities.

Once I got a booking to collect a fairly well-known Ukrainian singer from Bukovel. As far as I understood, she had just done a private corporate event and was on her way to the next one. Her son and sound engineer were with her.

Naturally, I was pleased.

Finally, I thought, we’ll talk about music.

I make music too — admittedly in a rather specific way. I do not play instruments, I cannot sing, and I do not have a band. I write lyrics, come up with the melodic idea and rhythm, and artificial intelligence adds the voice and instruments.

My virtual band has undeniable advantages: it is never late for rehearsals, never argues about the fee, and so far has not once gone on a drinking binge after a concert.

I had placed my greatest hopes on the sound engineer. I wanted to hear the opinion of someone who worked professionally with sound.

But the overnight corporate party proved stronger than my creative ambitions.

The sound engineer got into the car — and fell asleep. So convincingly that it was clear this was no longer sleep. It was a well-earned professional holiday.

Waking a man with the question,

“Tell me, what do you think about generative models in contemporary arranging?”

seemed inhumane.

So I quietly put on some of my own music in the background. Let the subconscious work, at least.

After a while, the singer listened more closely.

“Is this yours?”

“Mine. More precisely, the lyrics and concept are mine. The voice and instruments are AI.”

She was silent for a moment.

“We artists aren’t very fond of that sort of thing.”

“I understand. I’m not very fond of my own singing either. That is why humanity is still safe.”

She laughed.

“But seriously: I don’t have a band, I don’t play guitar, and I don’t have a voice. What I can do is write lyrics and hear a melody. Before, all of that would have stayed in a drawer. Now at least I can hear what it sounds like.”

“Send it to me. I’ll listen.”

Maybe she did.

Although I suspect it was the same professional “I’ll listen” that writers use when someone says:

“I’ve written something too. Can I send it to you?”

So the musical debate never happened. But not every run was memorable because of the passengers. Sometimes the route itself was enough for a story.

Once I had an ordinary Kyiv–Chernivtsi run. So ordinary that I probably would never have remembered it, if I had not found myself in Lima at five in the morning.

Pickup was scheduled for 5:00. As usual, I arrived early, opened the navigation app and began working my way toward the right building. At that exact moment, an air-raid alert was announced in Kyiv.

I looked at the screen — and Kyiv was gone.

I was in Lima.

Not on Kyiv’s Left Bank. Not somewhere on Lymanska Street. Lima. Capital of Peru.

Around me: pre-dawn Kyiv, a siren, familiar buildings and Ukrainian roads. Meanwhile the blue dot on my phone, without so much as blinking, insisted the Pacific Ocean was practically within reach.

Fortunately, I had downloaded an offline map of Kyiv in advance. War teaches you that the internet can disappear precisely when you need it most.

The map opened. Kyiv was still there. The street I needed was there too. Only one minor detail was missing — me.

Without proper GPS, the phone knew perfectly well where the building stood, but categorically refused to explain where I stood in relation to it.

I had to remember the days when drivers navigated not by a blue dot but by street names, house numbers and their own brains. I found the passenger on time.

The Kyiv–Chernivtsi run itself passed without incident. After an unexpected early-morning trip to Peru, Chernivtsi felt almost suburban.

The next journey returned me from geographical anomalies to human ones — and gave me a genuine cultural discovery.

Three young women were travelling from Lviv into the Carpathians for a friend’s wedding. The bride had booked the transfer both ways, so I was to take them there, sleep somewhere, and collect them the following day.

“Sleep somewhere” turned out to be a theoretical concept.

It was high season in the Carpathians. Everything was full: hotels, guesthouses, hostels. I suspect that if I had knocked on the door of a Hutsul cow shed, the cow would have answered:

“Sorry, bookings through Booking.com only.”

That was the first time in my life I slept in a transfer car.

But the girls themselves were the most interesting part.

After about thirty minutes, they appeared to forget completely that a driver is also a biological species equipped with ears. And I made a discovery that, at my age, I should probably have been ashamed to make: women discuss men with one another in much the same way men discuss women.

Sometimes considerably more specifically.

One of them was talking about a new boyfriend. About five years younger. At first everything had seemed perfect: young, athletic, energetic, in love.

Then the training began.

“I can’t take it anymore,” she complained. “He’s like a child! Everything has to be explained to him.”

“So teach him.”

“I’m sick of teaching him! I show him videos.”

Without meaning to, I tightened my grip on the steering wheel.

“What kind of videos?” her friend asked.

“Educational ones.”

“From YouTube?”

A short pause.

“Almost.”

I looked only at the road. I am a professional. Especially when the back seat turns into a distance-learning professional-development course with Pornhub serving as the teaching methodology department.

Her friends burst out laughing.

“I once had an older man,” she continued. “You didn’t have to show him anything.”

“Experience?”

“And a different attitude.”

“What kind?”

She thought for a second.

“He gave me diamond earrings.”

Now everyone went quiet.

At last I understood the difference between a young promising man and an experienced one: the first needs videos; the second already knows what to do. And brings earrings.

I drove the Mercedes on in silence.

The writer inside me, meanwhile, was diligently taking notes.

Not on my phone and not in a notebook. Just remembering.
