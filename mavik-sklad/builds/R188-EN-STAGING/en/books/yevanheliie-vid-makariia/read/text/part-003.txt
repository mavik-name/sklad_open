CHAPTER TEN — OF IMMORTALITY AND THE WAY

1 And Macarius said, sitting beneath a tree that knows no autumn:
“The Creator is not cruel, as those imagine who see only the ending. For death is not the ending, but a passage. And pain is not punishment, but remembrance.”
2 For the Creator is not He Who breaks, but He Who shapes.
3 Not He Who rages, but He Who waits.
4 Not He Who takes away, but He Who leads.
5 And the voice spoke in the silence:
“I made you not as a shadow that vanishes, but as a light that does not go out. For your soul is not of flesh, but of Me. And as I live, so does it live. Death is only the laying aside of a garment, not disappearance.”
6 And Macarius said:
“Do not fear death, for it is not an ending, but a mirror. And do not weep for the body, for the body is like a tent: it serves, but does not speak. The soul is like a voice without boundaries.”
7 And they asked him:
“Why then do we die, if we were made immortal?”
8 And he answered:
“Because your way is like a river: it may run long if you do not dam it with stones. And the stones are choice, and word, and deed. Every day is a chance either to remove a stone or to lay down another.”
9 And the Creator said:
“I do not shorten your way — you narrow it yourselves. I do not take away life — you yourselves leave it behind. I do not punish — I permit.”
10 And Macarius wrote:
“Let there be not fear in the heart, but understanding. For He Who made the angels made you also. Like the angels, you have light; but as men, you have choice.”
11 And he said:
“Let everyone who reads this remember: life is not a gift merely given, but a way that is built. Death is not an ending, but a door. And beyond the door is He Who waits.”

CHAPTER ELEVEN — OF THE TWO FACES OF DEATH

1 And Macarius said as he looked upon a flame that did not burn, but gave light:
“As day has night, as a beginning has an end, as love knows hatred — so death has two faces. For this world is like a mirror that reflects duality, but does not divide truth.”
2 And the voice within him said:
“Death is not an ending, but a state. For some, it is passage to Me. For others, a return to flesh, that they may walk again the way that was lost, forgotten, or despised.”
3 And Macarius said:
“If a man walks the way of the Creator, his death shall be like an ascent: clear, conscious, pure. For his spirit shall rise like the smoke of incense and find itself in the Eternal Kingdom, where there is no time, for all is presence.”
4 But he who has stepped from the way, who has forgotten reason and listened only to desire, and despised silence —
5 He shall not perish, but return.
6 And his soul shall descend again to the earth like a drop that has not yet become the sea.
7 And he shall become a child, that he may learn again to be silent, to love, to notice.
8 And so it shall be until the way becomes his flesh and truth becomes his blood.
9 And they asked him:
“How many times shall this be repeated?”
10 And Macarius answered:
“Only the Creator knows the number of circles. For samsara is not a prison, but a school. And He Who waits does not demand; He gives time and possibility.”
11 And he wrote:
“Death of the body is inevitable. But death of the soul is only a state of forgetting. For we were made like angels — immortal, lucid, able to walk the road of light. It depends only on us whether that road shall be straight, or a circle without end.”
12 And he said:
“Let everyone who reads understand: the Creator does not punish — He shapes. He does not close the door — He waits beside the threshold.”

CHAPTER TWELVE — OF ABRAHAM AND THE SACRIFICE THAT DID NOT BECOME DEATH

1 And Macarius said as he stood before an altar where there was no fire:
“True sacrifice is not killing, but readiness. And blood is not the proof of faith, but the silence in which faith lives.”
2 For the word of the Creator came — not like thunder, but like a whisper passing through the bones — to a man:
“Take your son, your only one, born of the promise, and bring him to the mountain. And there lay him upon an altar that has no name. For I would see whether your faith is stronger than your heart.”
3 And the man rose — not like a stone that feels nothing, but like a sword cutting its own shadow.
4 And he took his son — not as a lamb, but as a gift beyond price.
5 And three went together: the man, the son, and silence. And every step was like the blow of a heart that does not ask why.
6 And the son asked, “Where is the lamb?” And the father said, “The Creator sees more than we do.”
7 And there was no lie in those words — only faith that knew pain.
8 And when the man raised his hand, there was no anger in it, but trust.
9 And the voice passed through the air like music through water:
“Do not touch the boy, for I have seen that your heart is pure, and your faith is like a mountain that does not tremble beneath the wind.”
10 And a lamb appeared — not as the sacrifice, but as a substitute. For sacrifice is not blood, but readiness.
11 And the man wept — not from fear, but from release. For he understood: sacrifice is not loss, but the giving of oneself.
12 And Macarius said:
“Let everyone who has something dearest to him not be afraid, but place it in the hands of Him Who gave it. For the Creator does not require death — He requires faith.”

CHAPTER THIRTEEN — OF THE MAN AND THE STONE THAT SPEAKS

1 And a man went up upon a mountain that breathed clouds, and there was with him neither people nor fear — only solitude that hears the Creator.
2 And He Who has no image said:
“I shall write My words not upon sand, but upon stone, that neither time nor desire may erase them.”
3 And the man took the stone — not as a weapon, but as a heart ready to receive fire.
4 And the finger of the Creator touched what was hard, and hardness became a word.
5 And there were ten words — not as commands, but as a way leading not to fear, but to freedom.
6 And the first word was like a root:
“You shall have no other gods, for I am not one among many; I am the one source.”
7 And the second was like a mirror:
“You shall make no image for yourself, for I am not in stone; I am in the breath that gives life.”
8 And the third was like silence:
“You shall not speak My Name in vain, for My Name is not a sound, but a presence.”
9 And the fourth was like rhythm:
“Remember the day of rest, for even I rested, and you are not a slave to time.”
10 And the fifth was like roots:
“Honour father and mother, for through them you came, and through them you go onward.”
11 And the sixth was like a boundary:
“You shall not kill, for life is not your possession, but My breath.”
12 And the seventh was like a covenant:
“You shall not betray, for love is not a game, but a covenant.”
13 And the eighth was like truth:
“You shall not steal, for all you have is gift, not plunder.”
14 And the ninth was like light:
“You shall not bear false witness, for a word is a sword, and with it one may kill.”
15 And the tenth was like depth:
“You shall not desire what belongs to another, for a heart longing for what is another’s cannot hear itself.”
16 And when the man descended, his face shone, for it was not he who spoke, but the stone in his hands.
17 And Macarius said:
“The commandments are not a wall, but a door. Not shackles, but keys. For they serve not as restriction, but as an opening — of the way that leads not to submission, but to dignity.
18 For every ‘you shall not’ is the guardian of your ‘yes’, and every boundary is a shore, lest you drown in your own freedom.
19 For He Who gave the stone does not desire control, but encounter. And the law is not a burden, but an invitation to be human in the light of His face.”
